## challenge:aneutronic-fusion

**Current label (kept)**
Inching: first boron fusion on a private tokamak; plasmas still far below the ~1 billion °C needed

**Current rationale**
In September 2026 the Chinese company ENN produced proton-boron fusion reactions in its own tokamak, about 100 million a second, by firing fast protons through a much cooler plasma, and a peer-reviewed Japanese-American study showed radio-wave heating can stretch out such beam-driven reactions; Helion reported 150 million °C in its prototype. None of this is close to net energy, which these fuels reach only near a billion degrees: a peer-reviewed critique argued that Helion's helium-3 gain estimates leave out a heat loss larger than the fusion power itself, Helion said its first plant will not reach full power in 2028, and TAE, the main proton-boron developer, disclosed sustained temperatures of only 75 million °C and doubt about its survival as a going concern; these weigh on the leading programmes' prospects but no experiment failed. ENN aims to start experiments on its larger EHL-2 machine by end-2027, and Helion's Orion plant is to begin initial operations in 2028.

**Proposed rationale**
In September 2026 the Chinese company ENN produced proton-boron fusion reactions in its own tokamak, about 100 million a second, by firing fast protons through a much cooler plasma: a detectable trickle, nowhere near energy gain, and a repeat on a company machine of what TAE had already shown with a Japanese national laboratory; a peer-reviewed Japanese-American study found that radio-wave heating modestly raises the yield of such beam-driven reactions, and Helion reported 150 million °C in its prototype, burning conventional deuterium-tritium fuel. None of this is close to net energy, which these fuels reach only near a billion degrees, and the year's setbacks hit the leading programmes' schedules, finances and projections rather than their experiments: a peer-reviewed critique argued that Helion's helium-3 gain estimates leave out a heat loss larger than the fusion power itself, Helion no longer promises full power from its first plant in 2028, and TAE, the main proton-boron developer, disclosed sustained temperatures of only 75 million °C and doubt about whether it can keep operating. ENN aims to start experiments on its larger EHL-2 machine by end-2027, and Helion's Orion plant is to begin initial operations in 2028.

## challenge:deep-geothermal

**Current label (kept)**
Advancing: first utility-scale enhanced geothermal plant online (33 MW); 10–20 km drilling unproven

**Current rationale**
On 30 September 2026 Fervo's Cape Station in Utah became the first utility-scale enhanced geothermal plant in commercial operation, selling 33 MW made by pumping water through artificially fractured hot rock, while a 3 MW pilot in Texas and the UK's first geothermal plant, fed by a 5 km well, also began supplying their grids; the setback came in Germany, where Eavor's closed-loop plant was stopped half-built after cementing failures and rarely exports power. The deep, superhot rock that would make geothermal a fusion-scale alternative is still out of reach: these wells go down about 6 km, and the millimeter-wave drill meant for 10–20 km is at about 1 km by its developer's own account. Fervo expects two more 33 MW units by January 2027 and 400 MW in 2028, and Quaise is drilling a superhot confirmation well in Oregon for a 50 MW plant planned as early as 2030.
