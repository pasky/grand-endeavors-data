## milestone:the-first-watt

### 1. Literal target and gap

Framework: **“First commercial fusion plant delivers sustained electricity to a public grid.”** The ledger establishes no such plant and no verified net-electricity precursor. The count of evidenced qualifying plants is zero versus one required; a multiplicative gap from zero is undefined. A pulse of fusion energy, a long plasma discharge, a power purchase agreement, or site earthworks is not sustained public-grid electricity. One successful commercial plant suffices; no fleet-wide success or universal reliability threshold is imposed, but sustained operation of that plant is essential.

The closest quantitative physics result is **NIF Q=4.13, 8.6 MJ fusion / 2.08 MJ incident laser energy on 2025-04-07**, event `2025-04-07-nif-q-4-13-rv` (verified). This is target gain, not electrical gain. Illustratively, with 10% wall-plug laser efficiency and 40% thermal-to-electric conversion, both engineering assumptions rather than observations, gross electrical breakeven would require Q > 1/(0.10 × 0.40) = 25 even before auxiliary loads: 25/4.13 = 6.05×, log2(6.05) = 2.60 doublings. Repetition rate, consumable targets, heat extraction and auxiliaries remain additional capability dimensions, not measured by Q. Helion uses a different direct-conversion path, so that laser calculation is a gap illustration, not a conversion of NIF Q into Helion readiness.

### 3. Model, floors, and reporting gaps

Use a **reference-class / discounted programme-schedule mixture**, not a fabricated gain trend. Stage-of-development and concept choice dominate uncertainty. Verified Orion earthworks/permitting and NIF/TAE/magnet achievements establish measurable progress; announcements alone do not determine the colour.

A schedule-duration multiplier F follows lognormal(log(2), 0.40): median **2×**, central 80% **1.20–3.34×**. This is an elicited deep-tech schedule prior, motivated by Helion's missed demonstration and ITER's nine-year slip. The ledger does not contain original promises and completion dates sufficient to call 2× a measured multiplier. Applying the discount to the remaining project duration at its evidenced 2025 state avoids inventing an original PPA announcement date. No elapsed unobserved time to October 2026 is added to the multiplier.

One joint unconditional distribution, with mutually exclusive outcomes (probabilities are assessor judgments, not empirical frequencies):

1. **50%**: the early direct-conversion/Helion class delivers first. T = max(2028, 2025.56 + (2028 − 2025.56) F). Conditional median **2030.44**.
2. **35%**: an early-2030s ARC-like programme delivers first, after the fast path fails or loses the race. T = max(2030, 2025.65 + (2032.5 − 2025.65) F). Conditional median **2039.35**. TAE adds a candidate but is not treated as an independent guaranteed success.
3. **8%**: only a successor-generation/large-programme route succeeds. T follows triangular(min **2040**, mode **2055**, max **2080**). ITER's 2039 D-T target anchors the earliest edge; 2055/2080 express judgmental engineering and commercialisation uncertainty, not an ITER grid commitment.
4. **7%**: no qualifying commercial public-grid plant by 2100 (T = infinity).

These scenario weights express substantial technology risk while assigning 85% to the currently pursued commercial generation. The 50% fast-path weight is explicitly uncertain because no net-electricity precursor is established; it is not a measured success probability. It is not multiplied by an additional generic benchmark-to-reality or reliability discount. Probability of success must enter the entire distribution, not just its upper quantile. The lower bounds 2028/2030 prevent a lognormal left tail earlier than the programmes' own construction plans; no extra generic commissioning years are stacked on the median. Sustained operation is included in completion of a qualifying commercial plant, not equated to a first pulse.

The fusion engineering events have no substantively newer 2026 results after the records published 2026-01-03. This is an **unmeasured gap**, not a measured stall. I checked all effective fusion events for a faster report of the same outcome: none establishes fusion electrical export, a newer Q measurement, or commercial availability. Later household/wholesale prices and geothermal grid delivery are different outcomes and cannot fill this gap. There is no measured exponential capability rate to extrapolate; if a gain history is gathered, it must be fitted in log space rather than inferred from roadmaps.

### 3. Growth model and current-rate check

Fit log(LCOE) against calendar year, not an additive $/year line. Offshore wind is the main capital-intensive construction analogue; onshore/PV are cross-checks rather than substitutes chosen for their faster declines. Use the latest five calendar years **2020–2024**, with **three** measured points, for its central rate; all six points provide a historical sensitivity. Do not interpret 2024–October 2026 as flat or restart the technology clock in 2026. The extrapolation is a cost-learning process after the future first fusion plant, not an assertion that hypothetical fusion costs already fall while there are no plants.

Exact log-OLS results (log units/year; SE is descriptive with tiny samples):

| Metric | 2010–2024 slope | Compound annual change | 2020–2024 slope | Compound annual change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Offshore | −0.07610689 (SE .00805047) | −7.3283% | **−0.02979713** (SE .00998613) | **−2.9358%** |
| Onshore | −0.09106146 (SE .00903898) | −8.7038% | −0.03430028 (SE .02842108) | −3.3719% |
| PV | −0.16293502 (SE .01779367) | −15.0354% | −0.06153327 (SE .01372910) | −5.9678% |

Thus the current offshore measured rate r = **0.0297971293/year** is central, not the faster 2010–2024 rate. Its cost half-life is ln(2)/r = **23.26 years**. There are no cumulative installed-capacity observations here from which to claim a measured Wright learning rate. The equivalent Wright form, **only if** fleet capacity doubles every Td years, is C(N) = C0 (N/N0)^−b, LR = 1 − exp(−r Td). For an illustrative Td = **3 years**, LR = **8.5512%** per doubling and n = ln(C0/100)/[−ln(1−LR)]. At C0=170, n=**5.9360** doublings and n Td = **17.8080 years**. Td cancels because only the observed temporal analogue rate, not a fabricated fusion fleet series, sets the central forecast.

No faster-reporting comparable fusion-LCOE series or more recent IRENA observation appears in the effective events or registry. Wholesale EU/US observations to H1 2026 and retail observations to Q3 2026 measure market/final-user prices, not lifetime production cost of new fusion plants. They cannot set the learning slope. The last-point gap adds uncertainty, not zero-growth points. The newer 2022 and 2024 offshore observations themselves, rather than that gap, make the recent rate slower than the long-run fit.

### 4. Time-to-target calculation and cross-checks

Central deterministic proxy: C(t)=C0 exp[−r(t−T_FW)]; crossing time = **T_FW + ln(C0/100)/r**, evaluated infinitesimally beyond equality for the strict threshold. Using T_FW≈2034.13:

- C0=150: **13.6075 years**, around **2047.74**.
- C0=170: **17.8080 years**, around **2051.94**.
- C0=190: **21.5408 years**, around **2055.67**.

No extra follow-on-order or regulatory lag is stacked on this learning interval; repeat construction and its financing are already implicit in the analogue. The no-fleet/no-learning possibility goes into explicit probability mass, not an arbitrary number of years.

The full joint forecast uses the **same First Watt realization** in each draw, then C0 ~ lognormal(log(170), **0.25**) ($123–234 central 80%, about 1.7% probability below $100), r ~ lognormal(log(0.0297971293), **0.55**) (central 80% roughly **0.0147–0.0603/year**), and an independent **20%** no-effective-cost-learning branch. If C0<100 the plant qualifies without learning, even in the no-learning branch; otherwise that branch's time is infinity. All other draws use T = T_FW + max(0, ln(C0/100))/r. These spreads and the 20% risk are explicitly elicited proxy-transfer uncertainties, not confidence intervals from fusion measurements. The fission cost range and lack of plant experience motivate a meaningful failure probability, not a measured 20% frequency. C0 draws below $150 allow nonmagnetic/direct-conversion concepts outside Lindley's scope.
