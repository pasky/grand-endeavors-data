# fusion assessment 20261028T184845Z (as of 2026-10-28): working notes

This run assessed three open challenges only: challenge:d-t-fusion, challenge:aneutronic-fusion
and challenge:deep-geothermal. Under RUBRIC.md §7 challenges get no ETA, so this file has no
"## milestone:" sections. None of the three had a previous assessment, so rule 7 had nothing to
carry over.

## Method (all three challenges)

- Window: (2025-10-28, 2026-10-28]. An event counts when its date overlaps the window, with a
  partial date ending no later than its publication date (`ledger.py` `in_momentum_window`).
- Evidence: the effective fusion events tagged with the challenge and published <= 2026-10-28.
  Superseded records were excluded and their `-rv`/`-r2` replacements used instead. Legacy records
  were read for context only. I also scanned the untagged window events; none was a result on any
  of these challenges.
- Events dated only by year ("2025") could not be placed inside the window, so none is counted as
  an advance or setback. Examples: Fervo's 2025 drilling record and Quaise's 100 m mm-wave hole.
- Construction progress, design reviews, licences, funding, PPAs and plans are not advances
  (§7). They are cited as context and feed the prospects.

## challenge:d-t-fusion: Advancing (green)

- Substantive advance: `2026-01-01-east-density-free-regime`. A Science Advances paper (peer
  reviewed) reports that EAST held stable densities of 1.3–1.65× the Greenwald limit. That is a
  new operating regime on "plasma stability", one of the framework's named challenges. It was
  reached in ohmic start-up, not under burning-plasma conditions.
- Non-substantive advances:
  - NIF achieved ignition for the 11th time, at 7.9 MJ and gain ~3.8, below its 2025 record.
  - Tokamak Energy's Demo4 magnet reached 13.7 T, up from 11.8 T.
  - ST40 reached a triple product of ~8e18, roughly 3 orders of magnitude short of ignition.
  - HH70 held a 1,337 s pulse.
  - Zap reached 2.6 GPa, up from 1.6 GPa.
  - General Fusion's LM26 reached ~1.1 keV.
  - MAST-U reached its highest stable plasma pressure.
  - Pulsed-power and laser drivers advanced: Pacific Fusion's 440 GW pulser, Sirius's 3,000
    shots and Xcimer's Phoenix.
  - Inertia/LLNL cut D-T layering to 2–3 h.
  - Coil-free magnetized targets were demonstrated.
  - First Light's FLARE compression stage was demonstrated.
  - Marathon's isotope enrichment was reported without enrichment factors.
- Setback: SPARC had 8 of its 18 TF magnets installed on 2026-09-22, against a plan of all 18 by
  the end of summer. This is a slip of months. It does not outweigh the advances, and ITER is now
  ahead of its own schedule.
- What still blocks: no demonstrated result yet on tritium breeding (TBR>1), on neutron-resistant
  materials, or on a 10 Hz repetition rate with cheap targets.

## challenge:aneutronic-fusion: Inching (yellow)

- Advances, none substantive:
  - ENN produced p-B11 reactions on EXL-50U at ~1e8 α/s. This is a first for a private device,
    but beam-driven p-B11 reactions had already been shown at LHD, and the result is far from gain.
  - A NIFS–TAE paper in Nuclear Fusion (peer reviewed) shows ICRF raising the beam-driven p-B11
    yield. The gain is modest: the alpha count decays in 27.2 ms with ICRF against 19.8 ms without.
  - ENN reported the first H-mode in a hydrogen-boron plasma (company-reported).
  - Helion's Polaris reached 150 M°C and showed measurable D-T fusion (company-reported).
- Setbacks:
  - The Lackner et al. comment (peer reviewed) argues that ion-to-electron power transfer exceeds
    the D-He3 fusion power by more than 10× at 100 keV.
  - Helion now says Orion will not reach full power in 2028; full power is put at 2029–2030.
  - TAE has a going-concern doubt. Its S-4 also discloses that its Norm device has sustained only
    75 M°C.
- Judgment: this was a close call between Inching and Setback. I chose Inching because the
  measured quantities moved forward while the setbacks are a critique, a schedule slip and a
  financial problem. No experiment failed and no programme was cancelled for technical reasons.

## challenge:deep-geothermal: Advancing (green)

- Reading: "deep geothermal" as the mid-term fusion alternative includes enhanced geothermal
  systems (wells of ~4–6 km). The framework's detail, mm-wave/plasma drilling to 10–20 km, is
  reported as what still blocks.
- Substantive advance: Fervo's Cape Station reached commercial operation on 2026-09-30
  (`2026-09-30-fervo-cape-station-commercial-operation`). It is the first utility-scale EGS plant
  in commercial operation, at 33 MW net.
  - Corroboration is a judgment call. The ledger's sources for it are only Fervo's releases.
  - I accepted it because grid synchronization and a contractual commercial-operation date involve
    third parties (the grid operator and the PPA buyer).
  - Sage's 3 MW pilot, reported by a third party (Canary Media), independently shows EGS
    delivering grid power.
  - Sensitivity: if Cape Station is not counted as substantive, the verdict is Inching (yellow).
- Non-substantive advances:
  - Sage's 3 MW pilot.
  - United Downs: the UK's first geothermal power plant, fed by a >5 km well.
  - Fervo's Sawtooth 7 well: 19,448 ft in 21 days.
  - Fervo's Blanford appraisal well: 555°F.
  - Mazama's Athena well: 10,350 ft in 15 days.
  - Quaise's mm-wave drill "approaching 1 km", on the company's word only.
  - GA Drilling's NexTitan dual-unit field test.
- Setback: Eavor's Geretsried project was underbuilt after cementing failures, and Eavor exited
  the operator role. Because of this, its December 2025 "first power" is not counted as an
  advance. The setback does not outweigh Cape Station.
