## MAJOR findings

### 1. the-10m-solopreneur — assumed recurring-revenue inputs are presented as measured

The revenue model does not consistently preserve the ledger's distinction between revenue and recurring revenue. The input register labels path B's 15-month doubling time **measured**, and starts that path at $2.7M as though it were ARR. However:

- `2025-01-01-pieter-levels-3m-arr-rv` explicitly corrects the earlier ARR claim to approximately $3M **revenue**, without a period or recurring share. The previous assessment correctly registered annualization as assumed. The current notes acknowledge that missing information in the ladder, then use the point as a recurring-revenue baseline to derive the 15.4-month rate.
- `2026-09-23-pieter-levels-business-revenue-200-250k-month` establishes $200–250K monthly **business revenue**, not its recurring share. It supports annualizing total revenue to $2.4–3.0M, not equating the $2.7M midpoint with ARR.
- Path A's register also labels its 1.2/3.2-month p10/p50 measured. The 1.2-month calculation mixes the March $3M ARR claim with May's $9.7M headline cash-flow run rate; `2026-05-23-polsia-run-rate-critique-subscriptions-4-6m` separates $4.64M subscriptions from one-off packs and pass-through. The notes disclose that treating March's $3M as comparable recurring revenue is an assumption, but the register does not carry that qualification. Assigning these rates to forecast quantiles is itself assumed.

This affects the modeled quantity, not just terminology: changing only path B's recurring starting level from $2.7M to $1.35M gives p50 **2030.47** and p90 **2040.93**, versus **2030.08/2037.02**. This is a diagnostic, not a proposed estimate of the true recurring share.

**Fix:** distinguish measured total revenue from assumed recurring share and annualization; explicitly register the cross-company frontier-growth mapping and the path-A comparability/quantile assumptions. Supply recurring-share/period sensitivities or evidence establishing comparable ARR. Update `calc`, the register and the rule-7 explanation accordingly. Do not restore the corrected-away ARR claim by citing its replacement id.

### 2. the-linux-kernel-patch — the central mapping still uses the benchmark the audit confined to a cross-check

The notes' kernel §1 derives the central **360-hour** requirement as the geometric mean of **233 hours** for MirrorCode gotree and **554 hours** for Ladybird. This is not merely an optimistic-bound use of MirrorCode: its effort estimate supplies half the central calibration in log space. Yet the audit-resolution section says the oracle benchmarks are not central, and audit `2026-10-16-eta-basis/report.md` §3 explicitly restricts benchmarks with human-time estimates, including MirrorCode, to optimistic bounds rather than the central mapping for review-gated milestones.

The claimed reference class of **two size-matched ≥10k-line contributions** is also overstated. `2026-04-10-mirrorcode-opus46-reimplements-gotree` reports a **16,905-line input program**, but the produced Rust implementation has **7,644 lines** and is not a maintainer-accepted critical-infrastructure contribution. Source-program size is not the milestone's submitted diff size. Its estimated human effort can inform an analogue, but does not establish the stated qualifying class.

**Fix:** derive the central mapping from the precursor ladder and an appropriately sourced contribution-effort class consistent with the audit. Keep gotree's oracle-checked result as an optimistic cross-check, or obtain an explicit revision of the audit restriction. Correct the class membership/count and label any remaining transfer assumption. Recompute and attribute the resulting changes; do not claim this audit finding is already fixed.

### 3. the-linux-kernel-patch — a few search examples are turned into a measured size ceiling

The ladder and `calc` treat **~3,950 lines** as the largest substantive AI-assisted Linux contribution, conclude the ≥10k rung is unreached, and derive a censored requirement **above 7.930 horizon-hours**, or **>1.08 additional doublings** beyond the Node.js rung. **Fix:** describe these as the largest examples inspected, not a measured ceiling. Obtain an adequate search of qualifying contributions/series before using absence as a capability lower bound, or explicitly treat that rung as unknown and revisit its numerical role. The owner-requested censored-rung method does not make an incomplete search exhaustive.

### 4. the-nature-author — part of the p90 move is unreported simulation noise, not the attributed input changes

The record attributes the entire p90 move **2034 → 2046** to the driver, ladder, rung uncertainty, autonomy deficit and lag, while describing failure scenarios as unchanged. The implementation nevertheless replaces the previous milestone/failure random streams (`20261015`) with new streams (`20261016`). With every **new timing draw and probability held fixed**, restoring only the previous failure masks changes the final p90 from **2046.7759 to 2045.53**. Thus more than a year of the reported tail date depends on this unattributed numerical change.

There is a second confound within the attribution table: `m_sd=0` skips drawing the autonomy normal, shifting the subsequent lag and failure draws. The reported autonomy step is **2041.95 → 2046.06** (+4.11 years). Keeping the final streams but fixing `m=0` gives **2042.32**, so the isolated autonomy effect is **+3.74**, with **+0.37** instead coming from the stream shift. No evidence or audit finding calls for changing failure realizations.

The fragility is substantial: analytically mixing the unchanged failure probability (approximately **0.9080**) into the same 10,000 conditional timing draws gives p90 **2043.97**, versus **2046.78** from the sampled failure masks (realized success share **0.9035**). This diagnostic is not a replacement forecast, but shows why attributing the whole tail move to substantive inputs is misleading under rule 7.

**Fix:** use stable, separate streams for unchanged inputs and controlled attribution comparisons; preferably integrate the independent failure mass analytically over the canonical driver draws. Check numerical stability without privately resampling/refitting the shared driver. Recompute the attribution, identifying any numerical-method correction separately from evidence/model changes.
