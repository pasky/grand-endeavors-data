## challenge:programmable-immunity

**Current label (kept)**
Advancing: first approved regulatory T-cell therapy; deaths paused some autoimmune CAR-T trials

**Current rationale**
In June 2026 the FDA approved the first therapy built from regulatory T cells, the immune system's own brakes, after a randomized trial in which it raised one-year survival free of chronic graft-versus-host disease (donor immune cells attacking the patient after a stem-cell transplant) from 38% to 78%; one-off 'immune resets' with engineered CAR-T cells kept most people with severe lupus and related diseases off immune drugs, and a ten-year follow-up found a single CAR-T infusion still holding lymphoma off in about a third of patients, though a fifth were diagnosed with another cancer over the decade. Kyverna expects to complete its US filing for a CAR-T in stiff person syndrome in late 2026.

## challenge:safe-in-vivo-rejuvenation

**Current label (kept)**
Inching: senolytics reduced liver scarring; lasting in-body rejuvenation remains unproven

**Current rationale**
In October 2026 a small double-blind trial found that the drug pair dasatinib plus quercetin, which clears worn-out 'senescent' cells, reduced liver scarring in 8 of 17 patients with fatty liver disease versus 1 of 14 on placebo, and Life Biosciences' regulated trial of a gene therapy that partly reprograms eye cells inside the body reported no serious side effects in its first three glaucoma patients after two months. Whether cells can be reset inside the human body safely and lastingly is still unshown: the reprogramming data cover three patients over two months, prolonged reprogramming kills mice through liver failure, and the best senolytic drugs leave some senescent cells alive. Life Biosciences is moving to a higher dose, and NewLimit plans to take a liver reprogramming therapy into human trials in 2027.
