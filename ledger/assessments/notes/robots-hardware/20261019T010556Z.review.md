## MAJOR findings

### 1. milestone:robotaxi-saturation — consequential changes to the growth spread and floor are not attributed under rule 7

The previous working (`20261015T100057Z.md`) uses an assumed growth SD of **0.30/yr** and floor of **0.15/yr**. The new `robotaxi2` uses **0.25/yr** and **0.10/yr**. The change_note attributes the update to the new level, growth centre, supply stall and plateau probability, but does not identify these two old-to-new inputs or explain what invalidated their previous values. Describing a slower tail is not that attribution: lowering the centre already produces one, independently of changing its spread and floor.

These are not inconsequential numerical choices. Holding all other current inputs and the current seed fixed, the copied script gives:

| Growth uncertainty | p10 | p50 | p90 | p_achieved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Current SD 0.25, floor 0.10 | 2029.28 | 2030.99 | 2043.79 | 0.920 |
| Previous SD 0.30 only | 2029.18 | 2031.05 | 2045.99 | 0.920 |
| Previous floor 0.15 only | 2029.28 | 2030.99 | 2039.65 | 0.920 |
| Both previous values | 2029.18 | 2031.05 | 2040.57 | 0.920 |

Thus the unaccounted SD change crosses the **2031 → 2030 median-year boundary**, and the floor change contributes several years to p90. The notes disclose the new assumptions and sensitivities, but rule 7 additionally requires a valid reason to replace the previous assumptions. The fleet/share observations justify reconsidering the centre and mapping; they do not by themselves establish these particular replacement uncertainty parameters.

**Fix:** retain the previous spread/floor unless their replacement can be tied to specific new evidence, an identified error, rubric change or audit finding. If replacing them, explain that connection, give both old and new values, and include their effects in the change_note's attribution. Do not merely add a retrospective preference for the new judgments. Controlled reruns are in `review-work/diagnostics.txt`.

### 2. milestone:robotaxi-saturation — national licence restrictions are incorrectly described as having been temporary

The failure-scenario discussion in `basis.eta.calc` treats both Wuhan's suspension and China's licence pause as temporary, in support of the **0.02 permanent-regulatory-reversal scenario**. These are different restrictions:

- `2026-04-29-china-pauses-new-av-licences` says new licences, fleet additions, new test programmes and entry into new cities were stopped **for an unknown period**.
- `2026-08-03-apollo-go-wuhan-service-resumes` establishes partial resumption of existing Wuhan service in three districts after about four months.
- `2026-08-18-apollo-go-q2-2026-rides-fall-1m` reports affected operations beginning to resume; it does not say national licence issuance restarted.

The effective ledger therefore supports a temporary Wuhan operating suspension, not a resolved national licensing pause. The latter's resolution is an unsupported factual premise in the risk assessment.

**Fix:** distinguish the restrictions and leave the licence pause's duration unmeasured/unresolved. Reconsider the scenario using that actual state, or explain why the existing 0.02 remains appropriate despite it. A changed probability is not mandatory; a claim of resumed licensing requires an effective supporting record. Attribute any resulting quantile/probability moves.

### 3. milestone:the-fukushima-test — an unreported task outcome becomes an observed failure and moves the median

`2026-10-18-robot-warrior-fall-no-autonomous-humanoid-placing` establishes **29 teams, 5 autonomous entrants across morphology groups, 14 course tasks, and no reported autonomous-humanoid placing**. It does not establish how many autonomous humanoids attempted the valve, their valve-task outcomes, or that a successful autonomous repair necessarily earns a published placing.

The notes nevertheless treat zero autonomous-humanoid valve successes as observed. The script weights each draw by `sum(P(a) * (1 - p_joint)**a)`, with assumed attempt counts 0/1/2/3 weighted 0.25/0.35/0.25/0.15. An assumed attempt count does not fix the missing outcome: whenever `a > 0`, the likelihood still treats those attempts as known failures. The claimed implication from task success to published placing has no support in the event. The spring winner itself does not establish such an implication: its valve outcome was also unreported.

This is the entire final median-year move. With the updated autumn-venue probability and attempt rate retained, the reproduced results are:

- No fall-outcome conditioning: **2027.30 / 2027.80 / 2030.30**, p_achieved 0.950.
- Current conditioning: **2027.30 / 2028.30 / 2030.35**, p_achieved 0.950.

The **2027 → 2028** move is therefore falsely attributed to a measured failure. It violates §4.1 step 7's rule that an unreported outcome is unknown, not a failure, and rule 7's evidence requirement. The disclosed `a=0` sensitivity demonstrates the effect; it does not validate the base likelihood.

**Fix:** obtain round/task-level results establishing the unsuccessful attempts, or remove the zero-success likelihood from the base forecast. Keep the observed entrant counts and existence of an autumn competition as evidence for separately labelled opportunity assumptions. Any optional inference about unreported successes needs an explicit observation/reporting model rather than asserting failures the source does not report.
