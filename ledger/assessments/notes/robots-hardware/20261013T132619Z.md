## milestone:robotaxi-saturation

**Gap.** The quantity is robotaxis vs human-driven taxis in one major metro, measured as the ratio r = R/H. The target is r ≥ 1, which is odds 1 on a logistic share. The lead metro is San Francisco (Waymo). The ledger has no per-metro count, so the proxy is Waymo's system-wide fleet and ride volume. It tracks the target because the metro ratio is the local fleet divided by the local human fleet, and SF holds the largest share of the Waymo fleet.
- Ledger data points:
  - Waymo: 250k paid rides/week in Apr 2025 (2025-04-01-waymo-250k-weekly-rides, legacy); >450k/week with ~2,500 vehicles in 6 US cities in Dec 2025 (2025-12-01-waymo-450k-weekly-rides, legacy); 100M autonomous miles in Jul 2025; freeway rides in SF/LA/Phoenix from Nov 2025; Uber/Austin launch in Mar 2025.
  - China: Baidu Apollo Go >250k rides/week across 22 cities, 1,000+ vehicles in Wuhan (Nov 2025); Pony.ai 720+ vehicles and a Shenzhen city-wide permit (Oct 2025).
  - Others: Zoox ~50 vehicles; Tesla Austin 30–50 vehicles, still with safety riders at end-2025.
  - Setbacks: Cruise drag (2023) and shutdown (Dec 2024); Waymo school-bus recall (Dec 2025); Baidu Zhengzhou crash (Dec 2025).
- Background (not in the ledger, used for the denominators):
  - Waymo SF Bay fleet ~1,000 (700–1,300) in Dec 2025.
  - SFCTA "TNCs Today" counted up to ~5,700 ride-hail vehicles on SF streets at peak (2016 data). Post-COVID, taxis plus ride-hail on road at peak are ~5,000 (3,500–8,000).
  - ~1,500 SF taxi medallions, of which ~700–1,000 vehicles are active.
- The denominator depends on how "human-driven taxis" is read. Three readings, weighted:
  - A (0.6): taxis plus ride-hail, vehicles on road at peak. R on road ≈ 0.8 × 1,000 = 800 vs ~5,000, so r0 = 0.16 (0.10–0.26). Gap 6.3×, 2.6 doublings. Cross-check from ride share: Waymo SF ≈ 40% of 450k/wk ≈ 26k rides/day vs ~120k ride-hail+taxi trips/day gives a share of ~18%, odds ~0.2, consistent with A.
  - B (0.2): licensed taxis only. r0 = 0.9 (0.6–1.4), i.e. at or near parity, with only a published count missing.
  - C (0.2): all active ride-hail vehicles in a month (tens of thousands). r0 = 0.04 (0.025–0.07). Gap 25×.
  - The parenthetical "(L5 Transport Solved)" favours an inclusive denominator, so A carries most weight.

**Growth model.** The robotaxi share follows an adoption S-curve. On a logistic curve the odds s/(1−s) grow exponentially, and the target is odds = 1 (50% share). So no S-curve slowdown is due before the target. Deployed fleets and rides also grow exponentially.
- Central rate: the measured Waymo ride growth. 250k→450k/wk over Apr→Dec 2025 = 1.8× in 8 months = 2.41×/yr (ln 0.88/yr, doubling every 9.4 months).
- Two effects on SF odds roughly cancel, so the median uses the measured 0.88:
  - Slower: in 2026 the growth is spread over new metros (a named constraint, but 2025's measured rate already included two new metros, Austin and Atlanta).
  - Faster: the odds grow faster than R as human ride-hail loses share (d ln odds/dt = g/(1−s)).
- Spread: SD 0.30 on the log rate, floor 0.15.

## milestone:the-blue-collar-shift

**Gap.** The wording sets five conditions: a general-purpose robot works 8h+ shifts on 30 consecutive working days in a real workplace alongside humans, at ≥90% of required throughput, with no teleoperation and a disclosed average of ≤1 intervention per shift. Component by component:
- 8h+ shifts: shown.
  - AgiBot G2 at Longcheer: an 8h shift on 14 Apr 2026; 8 robots for 10h/day over six days in June 2026 (2026-04-14-agibot-g2-longcheer-8h-shift, 2026-06-28-agibot-longcheer-whole-section-8-robots, 2026-06-29-agibot-six-day-factory-livestream).
  - Figure 02 at BMW: 10h shifts in 2025 (2025-11-19-figure-02-bmw-production-run-r2).
  - Robotera: 2–3 shifts a day at China Post.
- 30 consecutive working days: claimed, not verified.
  - Galbot S1 at CATL: 7×24 for 3+ months since Mar 2026, "without remote control" (2026-06-24-galbot-s1-catl-24-7-three-months).
  - Figure at BMW: ~6 months of daily runtime, but independent reporting puts it at one robot at a time (2025-04-06-fortune-bmw-single-figure-robot, 2026-10-07-forbes-bmw-figure-scope).
  - Robotera at China Post: months on site.
- ≥90% throughput: shown at some sites.
  - Robotera 1,200/h vs a human average above 1,000/h (2026-05-31-robotera-china-post-sorting-1200-per-hour).
  - Dyna-2 at 106% of Din Tai Fung's shift requirement (2026-08-27-dyna-din-tai-fung-shift-throughput).
  - Figure 03 at 98.5% of a human over 10h, at its own facility (2026-05-18-figure-03-vs-human-10h-sort).
  - Xiaomi met a 76 s cycle at 90.2% success for 3h (2026-03-02-xiaomi-humanoid-3h-nut-station-90pct).
  - Not met: UBTech Walker S2 at 30–50% of a human (2026-01-26-ubtech-walker-s2-30-50pct-human).
- ≤1 intervention/shift, no teleop: **never disclosed**. This is the binding gap. Data on failure rates:
  - "Generally hour-level" humanoid MTBF, i.e. 2–8 failures per 8h shift (2026-04-14-agibot-humanoid-mtbf-vs-industrial).
  - AgiBot G2 MTBF raised to "two-shift" level (~16–20 h, so ~0.5 per shift); same event.
  - AgiBot claims "hundred-hour" MTBF, ~0.08 per shift (2026-03-24-agibot-mtbf-hundred-hours).
  - Longcheer: "minimal human intervention", downtime <4%, backed by a maintenance team.
  - Robotera needed six months of on-site support, including night supervision.
  - Figure 03: a 200h run "without a failure" (no catastrophic halt; dropped packages seen) (2026-05-22-figure-03-200h-autonomous-livestream).
  - Agility Digit: 65,000 h across customer sites, ~98% on-task accuracy (2026-09-15-agility-digit-65000-hours).
- Estimate: I0 = interventions per 8h shift at the best real-line deployment in mid-2026, lognormal median 1.2 (0.35–5). P(already ≤1) = 0.43. The gap at the median is log2(1.2) = 0.26 halvings.

**Growth model.** Reliability grows exponentially: the failure or intervention rate halves at a fixed interval.
- Driver for the median: Dyna's failure fraction against the quality bar fell from 25% (Jun 2025) to 7% (Aug 2026). That is 14 months × ln2 / ln(25/7) = a 7.6-month halving. It is the most current measured physical-task reliability series in the ledger, and it is a real customer deployment.
- Spread: 4–14 months.
  - Faster outliers (task-specific, p10 side): Figure barcode orientation 70% → 95% in 3 months (2025-06-07-figure-helix-logistics-scaling); Figure's 10 → 60h data scaling.
  - Slower side: components wear out under three-shift duty (harmonic reducers last 1.5–2 yr, 2026-06-02-harmonic-reducer-life-three-shift), which affects uptime more than interventions.
- METR cross-check: the 80% software task horizon doubles every 4.43 months (fit since 2025; 3.95 months since 2024.6). It is a different outcome, so it only confirms that 7.6 months is the conservative rate. It is not put in the median.
- Capability date = 2026.5 (the June 2026 runs) + max(log2 I0, 0) × halving time.
- Then a 30-working-day run: 30/250 = 0.12 yr.
- Then a disclosure and verification lag: lognormal, median 0.75 yr (0.25–2.5). This adjustment has its own evidence. In ~12 deployment events from 2024 to 2026 the deployers set or implied intervention targets (Figure/BMW: zero interventions per shift) and never published achieved counts. Independent reports (Fortune, Forbes, Economic Observer) show the proponent framing overstates the deployments.

**Time to target.**
- Point calculation: 2026.5 + 0.26 × 0.63 + 0.12 + 0.75 = 2027.5.
- MC: 1.9% of samples fall before made_at and are rejected (no qualifying event exists by made_at). Conditional p10 2027.01 / p50 2027.90 / p90 2030.03.
- p_achieved 0.92. Failure modes:
  - no deployer ever publishes a qualifying month-long intervention log in a verifiable form;
  - every qualifying run is disputed as not general-purpose (e.g. stationary arms) or as teleoperated.
- Unconditional: p10 2027.03, p50 2028.02, p90 2032.33. P(by + 5 yr) = 0.89.
- Result: **p10 2027, p50 2028, p90 2032**.

**Cross-checks.**
- Proponents: BofA expects humanoid "mass adoption" to start in 2028 (2025-04-30-bofa-humanoid-mass-adoption-2028-rv). UBTech aims for 80% of human efficiency by 2027. Aitu (Xinhua, 2026-07-23-xinhua-single-process-not-full-line) calls 2026–28 the validation period for single-process capability, and a single-process month-long shift fits that. Discounted 1.5–3×, these point to ~2028–2031, consistent with the median and p90.
- Reference class: a single limited chore in live production (Fortune, Apr 2025) became multi-day, multi-robot real-line livestreams with task logs (AgiBot, Jun 2026) in ~15 months. One more step, a month-long log with interventions counted, in ~1–1.5 years matches that pace.
- Previous ETA (assess:20261013T073431Z): p10 2027 / p50 2028 / p90 2031, p_achieved 0.95. The median is unchanged. The p90 moves 2031 → 2032 and p_achieved 0.95 → 0.92 because the disclosure tail is weighted more: no deployer has published a single intervention count in two years, and the Figure/BMW test has ended (Forbes, Oct 2026).

**Status.** Yellow. The median of 2028 is within 5 years, but the binding metrics (interventions, teleoperation) have no non-proponent corroboration. Not achieved: no verified event discloses ≤1 intervention per shift over 30 consecutive working days.

**Cruxes.**
- Earlier: AgiBot, Galbot/CATL, Robotera, Figure, Agility or Dyna publishing a month-long intervention log; a customer or auditor confirming ≤1 intervention per shift without teleop.
- Later: disclosed rates well above 1 per shift; deployments rolled back (as Figure at BMW); confirmed teleoperation; maintenance-team dependence turning out to equal interventions.
