## milestone:the-bend

**Current label:**
`Progressing: GHG +0.3% Jan–Jul 2026, definitive peak unconfirmed; ETA ~2036 (10-yr edge)`

**Proposed label:**
`Progressing: GHG roughly flat (+0.3% Jan–Jul 2026), peak not yet confirmed; ETA ~2036`

**Current rationale:**
Global greenhouse-gas emissions are roughly flat at a high: they rose 0.7% in 2025 excluding land use (EDGAR) and 0.3% in January–July 2026 (Climate TRACE); with land-use CO₂ included 2025 was about 0.2% below 2024, far short of the 1% test. But fossil-CO₂ growth keeps slowing decade by decade and clean power outgrew demand in 2025. Extrapolating that slowdown puts the physical peak around 2030 and its definitive recognition (peak at least 3 years back and the latest year at least 1% lower, plus a year of reporting) at a median of 2036, with a 95% chance by 2100: yellow, but right on the 10-year edge. Whether 2026's oil-shock dip becomes a lasting decline, and whether methane and other gases follow CO₂ down, would move this by several years either way.

**Proposed rationale:**

## milestone:the-balance

**Current label:**
`Distant: net GHG ~58 GtCO2e/yr, +0.3% Jan–Jul 2026 vs ≤2.9 (±5%) needed; P(by 2100) 28%`

**Proposed label:**
`Distant: net GHG ~58 GtCO₂e/yr, +0.3% Jan–Jul 2026; need ≤2.9; P(by 2100) 28%`

**Current rationale:**
Net human greenhouse-gas emissions are about 58 billion tonnes of CO₂-equivalent a year and still growing slightly, while durable carbon removal runs at roughly a thousandth of a billion tonnes. Reaching net emissions within 5% of zero (about 2.9 billion tonnes) needs a 20-fold cut plus several billion tonnes a year of removals; extrapolating today's slowing emissions growth and a removal industry growing like renewables did gives only a 28% chance by 2100, so there is no median year and the status is red. The answer hinges most on how big and how fast carbon removal can grow, and on whether methane and other gases fall along with CO₂.

**Proposed rationale:**
Net human greenhouse-gas emissions are about 58 billion tonnes of CO₂-equivalent a year and still growing slightly, while durable carbon removal runs at roughly a million tonnes a year. Getting net emissions down to about 2.9 billion tonnes (5% of today's gross emissions) needs a 20-fold cut plus several billion tonnes a year of removals; extrapolating today's slowing emissions growth and a removal industry growing as fast as renewables did gives only a 28% chance by 2100 — less likely than not even then, so red. The answer hinges most on how big and how fast carbon removal can grow, and on whether methane and other gases fall along with CO₂.

Why: "within 5% of zero" is not what the milestone says (5% of zero is zero) and "(±5%)" in the
label has no referent on the page; "a thousandth of a billion tonnes" is the same number said
awkwardly; "no median year" is forecasting jargon — what the reader needs is that success is
less likely than not even by 2100.

## milestone:the-ceiling

**Label:** leave as is.

**Current rationale:**
Long-term human-caused warming is about 1.3–1.4°C and still rising; the milestone needs it to stop below 2.0°C and stay at or below that peak for 10 years. On today's emissions trend the remaining 2°C carbon budget (about 1,020 billion tonnes of CO₂ at even odds) runs out around 2051, so a model of cumulative CO₂ plus the shorter-lived gases gives only an 18% chance by 2100: no median year, red. Most of the successful paths rely on methane falling fast enough to hold temperatures flat before CO₂ reaches net zero, which is the biggest uncertainty; this year's El Niño-boosted record months are weather on top of the trend, not the indicator itself.

**Proposed rationale:**

Most of the successful paths rely on methane falling fast enough to hold temperatures flat before CO₂ reaches net zero, which is the biggest uncertainty; this year's El Niño-boosted record months are weather on top of the long-term trend, not the measure itself.
