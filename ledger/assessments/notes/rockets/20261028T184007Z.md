# rockets assessment 20261028T184007Z (as of 2026-10-28)

This run covers only open challenges (closed-loop-life-support, isru-at-scale,
radiation-shielding). Under RUBRIC.md §7 they get no ETA forecast, so this file has
no milestone sections. The triage is recorded in each record's basis (advances,
setbacks, change_note), summarised here.

Window: 2025-10-28 .. 2026-10-28. Events: effective rockets events tagged with each
challenge, published <= 2026-10-28, superseded records excluded; legacy records were
used as context only. None of these challenges had a previous assessment (prev = null).

- **closed-loop-life-support: yellow (Inching).** Advances, none substantive: Tiangong
  at 100% O2 / >96% water closure after >5 years (the operator's statement relayed by
  People's Daily), ISS BPA 1,395 L over 1,079 days, Tiangong wheat in a second
  seed-to-seed cycle, and the Shennong Kaiwu-2 mini-ecosystem. Setbacks: the Kurita
  teardown found wear on JAXA's recycler, the ISS Sabatier upgrade was cancelled (ISS
  oxygen loop stays ~50% closed), and the Paragon HALO ECLSS contract ended with Gateway.
  These setbacks are programmatic or fixable, so they do not outweigh the advances.
- **isru-at-scale: yellow (Inching).** Advances, none substantive: MRE at 0.07 kg/h
  from 25 kg of simulant (a late-2024 test, first quantified in this window), CaRD
  producing CO from simulant, Chandrayaan-2 radar signs of subsurface ice, and the SWIM
  Mars ice map. Setbacks: ShadowCam found less surface ice in shadowed craters,
  Chang'e-7 slipped to 2027 or later, the PRIME-1 summary restates its 2025 failure,
  and a cost study found ISRU dearer than resupply except at maximum demand. I judged
  the setbacks not to outweigh the advances; this is the main judgment call, and
  weighting the setbacks more heavily gives Setback (red).
- **radiation-shielding: green (Advancing).** One substantive advance: the AstroRad/MARE
  paper in Science Advances (peer-reviewed, DLR-led), the first in-flight deep-space
  evaluation of a wearable shield, with ~58% / ~40% dose cuts in model solar particle
  events. Its limits: phantoms not crew, extrapolated spectra, and no effect on galactic
  cosmic rays. Other advances, not substantive: Artemis II dose of about 10 mGy, the
  GSI/FAIR GCR simulator, and Zhang et al. on solar-maximum timing. The setbacks were
  minor: the Artemis II shelter demo was shortened and K-RadCube returned no data.
