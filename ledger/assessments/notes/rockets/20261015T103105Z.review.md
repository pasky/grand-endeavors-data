## MAJOR 1 — milestone:the-homestead: the eight-year move is not justified by the cited rubric change

Record `2026-10-15-milestone-the-homestead-3`; notes lines 223–260 and 285–291. Previous record: `2026-10-15-milestone-the-homestead-2`, by `assess:20261015T073717Z`.

The previous working explicitly labelled US/China pursuit probabilities 0.35/0.45 as assumptions and supplied sensitivities. The new working changes US pursuit from 0.35 to 1/7, attributing this to v6's requirement for counted reference classes. On identical draws this changes p10 **2045 → 2046**, p50 **2075 → 2083**, and p_achieved **0.653 → 0.608** (stored 0.65 → 0.61). The resupply-only failure scenario increases from 0.190225 to 0.2325175.

The proposed count does not establish the replacement input. The notes first count **0 of 5 stations producing a meaningful share of food**, then describe that as **0 of 5 pursuing food closure** in the change explanation. Production outcomes and decisions to pursue closure are different quantities; the model already has a separate closure interval. The ledger's `2025-01-01-iss-food-fully-resupplied` establishes zero operational food production on ISS, with experiments, not the proposed five-programme pursuit denominator. The other station outcomes are assessor background, without a documented pursuit classification. Rule-of-succession smoothing and transferring Earth-orbit station outcomes to a future lunar programme are also assumptions, not a measured pursuit rate; the register instead labels 1/7 merely as a counted class.

V6 requires counts and an explanation of their connection to the scenario probability. It does not require replacing an existing disclosed judgment with the raw smoothed class rate, or forbid an assumed mapping without a measured uplift. The absence of a US surface-food programme was already known to the previous model; the notes expressly claim no new evidence. Consequently the stated attribution does not establish that 0.35 was contradicted by evidence or the rubric, as stability rule 7 requires. This is not an objection to using a reference class or to choosing a low probability in a first assessment.

**Fix:** retain 0.35 and the previous forecast while adding the counted-class discussion, explicitly assumed transfer, and 1/7 sensitivity; or supply evidence of actual pursuit/non-pursuit for a defined class and a valid rule-7 reason why it invalidates the previous input. Do not equate failure to produce food with failure to pursue production. Label the smoothing/transfer as assumed and keep the scenario-level calculation and sensitivities.

## MAJOR 2 — milestone:the-deluge: tanker-propellant sensitivity computes the wrong quantity

Record `2026-10-15-milestone-the-deluge`; notes lines 105, 120 and 536; also `basis.path`, `eta.calc`, and `eta.cruxes`.

The framework explicitly includes cargo **and tanker-delivered propellant**. The sensitivity describes JSR omitting propellant that constitutes half of future delivered mass, but executes `deluge(ton=0.5)`: it halves the extrapolated JSR mass and therefore reports **p50 2038**, later than the baseline **2035**. That is a subtraction of qualifying mass, not a correction for uncounted qualifying mass.

If the driver's projected JSR tally represents only half of the qualifying total, the same stylized scenario has qualifying mass `JSR_mass / 0.5`, or an equivalent JSR threshold of 50,000 t. Holding the canonical driver draws and other inputs fixed, `deluge(ton=2)` gives **p10 2031, p50 2033, p90 null, p_achieved 0.873**, rather than the reported sensitivity's **2034/2038/null, 0.871**. The exact timing of a future tanker contribution needs an assumed mapping; the direction of this accounting correction does not.

**Fix:** distinguish JSR-counted mass from framework-qualifying mass and recompute the sensitivity with an explicit future propellant share/ramp. Remove the later-ETA crux for an omitted positive contribution. Alternatively, explain a genuinely different physical slowdown scenario, without presenting it as a counting correction. Keep sampling `launch-mass`; no driver refit is needed. This finding concerns a wrong-event sensitivity, not a failure to reproduce the baseline arithmetic.
