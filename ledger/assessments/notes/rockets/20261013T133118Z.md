## milestone:the-deluge

**Quantity and gap.** The quantity is annual payload mass delivered to orbit (the milestone's own
quantity), from the JSR msatannual table. Event 2025-01-01-jsr-payload-mass-2025-rv2 gives the unit
as "payload tonnage" (tons; metric vs short not stated, a <10% ambiguity that is immaterial at 31x).
Latest value 3,194.0 t (2025) against 100,000 t/yr: gap 31.31x, or 4.97 doublings.

**Data** (`uv run core/ledger.py series rockets payload-mass-to-orbit --as-of 2026-10-13`), in t:
2011 687.1, 2012 351.5, 2013 328.8, 2014 331.1, 2015 347.1, 2016 337.4, 2017 394.5, 2018 388.9,
2019 399.2, 2020 572.0, 2021 797.4, 2022 1046.4, 2023 1462.8, 2024 2625.9, 2025 3194.0.
History: 1957-68 ramp to ~410 t, then a plateau of ~300-1,400 t until 2019.
Context events: SpaceX ~85% of 2025 mass (2025-01-01-spacex-share-of-mass-to-orbit, legacy),
i.e. ~2,715 t. SpaceX made 165 orbital flights in 2025 (the correction figure in
2025-01-01-spacex-167-launches-wd). In 2025 Starship flights 7, 8 and 9 each lost a stage
(-rv setbacks) and flights 10 and 11 succeeded (-rv), all suborbital tests with no operational payload.
New Glenn recovered its booster in Nov 2025 (-rv), and Guowang reached its 12th batch by Oct 2025 (-rv).
The NextBigFuture ~$100/kg Starship projection (-rv) is a projection only, and it does not move the status.

**Fits** (OLS on ln mass; script deluge.py, output inline):
- 2021-2025: slope 0.3695 +/- 0.0321 SE, so +44.7%/yr, doubling 1.88 yr, residual sd 0.102; t to 100 kt = ln(31.31)/0.3695 = 9.32 yr
- 2020-2025: 0.3574, so +43.0%/yr; 9.64 yr
- 2016-2020 (previous window): 0.1068, so +11.3%/yr; 32.3 yr
- 2016-2025: 0.2627, so +30.0%/yr; 13.1 yr
- 2011-2025 (log fit, the 2011 value is a high outlier): 0.1373, so +14.7%/yr; 25.1 yr
- Fixed rates: +21.6%/yr (the single 2024->25 step) 17.6 yr; +25% 15.4 yr; +30% 13.1 yr; +60% 7.33 yr
- Calendar totals at +44.7%/yr: 2033 61.4 kt, 2034 88.9 kt (short), 2035 128.6 kt, so the first 100-kt year on trend is 2035.

**Model and why.** Launch mass grows exponentially (§4.1 step 3), so I fit in log space. The
median uses the current 5-year rate. Two named constraints argue for a slowdown, but neither has
measured evidence. (a) Falcon 9 saturation: ~2.7 kt/yr from ~165 flights, and the +21.6% 2024-25
step is one observation. (b) The hand-off to Starship, a ~100 t reusable vehicle that is not yet
operational. Both widen the late side only.
Measurement gap: JSR publishes the calendar-year total after year-end, so 2026 is unmeasured, not
flat. I extrapolate from 2025. No faster-reporting series of the same outcome (a YTD-2026 tally,
2026 launch logs) is in the ledger, which is a gather request.

**Monte Carlo** (N=400k, seed 20261013). Log-slope split-normal: median 0.3695, 80% band
+22%..+60%/yr (sd 0.133 below, 0.078 above; lower edge = the 2024-25 step and the 2016-25 rate,
upper = Starship at operational cadence). Level noise sd 0.07 on the crossing year. P(never by
2100) 0.12, from the reference class: the 1957-68 ramp (~+43%/yr) stalled for ~50 years. A total
failure needs near-flat growth for a century, since +5%/yr from 2025 still reaches 100 kt by 2096.
Result: p_achieved 0.874. Unconditional p10 2033, p50 2035 (P by end-2035 0.506, by end-2036
0.584), p90 null. If achieved: 2033/2035/2042. P(by 2040) 0.751, P(by 2050) 0.845.

**Cross-checks.**
- Bottom-up driver (Starship cadence). 100 kt minus ~5 kt from other vehicles is ~95 kt from
  Starship-class flights, i.e. ~950 flights/yr at 100 t (633 at 150 t). Falcon flights grew
  26 (2020, assessor background, not in the ledger) to 165 (2025, ledger), slope 0.3696 (+44.7%/yr),
  the same rate as the mass trend. Starting from N0 operational flights in 2027:
  N0=10 at 100 t gives 12.3 yr (2039); N0=30 at 100 t 9.3 yr (2036); N0=30 at 150 t 8.3 yr (2035);
  N0=60 at 100 t 7.5 yr (2034-35). The trend median implicitly needs ~30 Starship flights' worth of
  mass in 2027. The starting level is unmeasured (no 2026 Starship data), so per step 3 this check
  widens the late side (P by 2040 only 0.75) and does not move the median.
- Reference class: Falcon 9 went from first booster landing (2015) to ~165 flights/yr (2025) in
  10 yr. Starship's first fully successful flights came in late 2025, so a comparable hand-off puts
  ~1,000 flights/yr around the mid-to-late 2030s, consistent with p50-p90.
- Proponents: no dated proponent claim for 100 kt/yr is in the ledger (only the $100/kg projection).
- Previous ETA (2026-10-13, v4): p_achieved 0.88, 2033/2035/null. Unchanged.

**Status.** p50 2035 <= 2036 (made_at + 10), with measurable progress on the own quantity, so yellow.
Not green: the median is more than 5 years out and there is no operational precursor at Starship
scale. Unchanged from prev, as rule 1 requires (no newer evidence).

**Cruxes.** Earlier: a JSR 2026 total >= ~4,600 t (on trend 4,622 t) or a partial-2026 tally above
trend; operational Starship payload (Starlink V3) flights at >= monthly cadence in 2026-27; ship
catch and reuse. Later: a 2026 total <= ~3,800 t (a second sub-trend year after +21.6%); further
Starship losses or grounding; no second reusable heavy launcher ramping.

## milestone:the-export

**Quantity and gap.** A product manufactured in space and sold commercially on Earth, beyond novelty.
This is a qualitative first, and one qualifying sale suffices, so I model the earliest of
competing paths. The measurable precursor is repeated in-space production with return to Earth.
Gap: 0 -> 1 qualifying sale. No event in the ledger shows a sale.

**Data points.**
- Varda: W-1 returned Feb 2024 under FAA Part 450 (2024-02-01-varda-w1-reentry-rv). W-5 launched
  28 Nov 2025 (legacy), so ~5 capsules in ~22 months. The company has done "a handful of missions"
  and names clinical trials as its next goal (2025-11-30-varda-five-missions-rv). No trial start
  is in the ledger.
- Flawless Photonics: >12 km of ZBLAN fiber drawn on the ISS, quality verification ongoing (legacy).
- Space Forge: semiconductor-manufacturing payload returned May 2025 (legacy).
- Redwire: bioprinted heart tissue and 14 PIL-BOX crystallization experiments (legacy).
- Platforms: Axiom first module welded, station NET 2028 (-rv); Vast Haven-1 targets 2027 (-rv).
- Market projection: $4.6B by 2030 and $62.8B by 2040 (MarketsandMarkets, -rv; a projection,
  context only).
- Launch supply: payload-mass-to-orbit +44.7%/yr (see the-deluge). There is no quantitative
  mapping from launch supply to sale odds, so it is context only.

**Model and why.** A qualitative first with one success sufficient, so the model is the earliest of:
- Path A, Varda pharma: a reformulation of an approved drug in a space-made crystal form leads to
  a marketed product or paid commercial supply. It has a structural floor of trials plus approval.
  Trial/IND start = made_at (2026.79) + lognormal(median 0.8 yr, 80% 0.2-2.5). The ~11 months since
  the Nov-2025 "next goal" statement are unmeasured (no 2026 events gathered), not a slip.
  Programme = lognormal(median 4.0 yr, 80% 2.5-7.0): 505(b)(2)-type bridging studies of 1-3 yr,
  ~1 yr review, and first commercial supply from orbit. P(path A ever succeeds) 0.5, allowing for
  a pivot to reentry services, trial failure, or no economics.
- Path B, materials (ZBLAN fiber, semiconductor seed crystals, other): a flat hazard of 6%/yr from
  2027. Anchor: ~40 years of microgravity materials work without a clear qualifying sale, at
  10-50x higher launch cost and without dedicated return capsules. Rule of three on ~40 yr gives
  an upper bound of <7.5%/yr under the old conditions. The cheaper access now (Varda 5 returns in
  2 yr) supports the upper half of that, so 6%.
- Common mode: P(space manufacturing never pays for an Earth product by 2100) 0.07.

**Monte Carlo** (export.py, N=400k, seed 7). Result: p_achieved 0.924. Unconditional p10 2029,
p50 2033 (P by end-2032 0.417, by end-2033 0.502), p90 2073 (the tail is the materials hazard
only). If achieved: 2029/2033/2053. P(by 2028) 0.054, 2030 0.195, 2031 0.308, 2036 0.639.
Sensitivity:
- P(A) 0.35: p50 2035; P(A) 0.65: 2033
- hazard 3%/yr: p50 2035, p_achieved 0.877 (p90 null); hazard 10%/yr: 2032
- common mode 0.15: p50 2034, p_achieved 0.844
- programme median 6 yr: p50 2035
Across all of these the median stays within 2032-2035.

**Cross-checks.**
- Reference class: reformulation programmes take ~5-8 yr from proof of concept to market. From
  Varda's first return (2024) that gives 2029-2032.
- Proponent: "first drug product late 2020s" (context, ~2028.5). Discounting the remaining ~2.6 yr
  by 1.5-3x gives 2030-2034.
- Previous ETA (2026-10-13, v4): p_achieved 0.92, 2028/2032/2078. Mine is one year later at the
  median: a 4-yr programme (vs 3.5) and a trial start 0.8 yr out. This is within noise of each
  other, and no new evidence is behind it.

**Status.** p50 2033 <= 2036, and there is measurable progress (repeated manufacture with return),
so yellow. Not green: the median is more than 5 years out, there is no precursor at commercial
scale, and every source is a proponent. Unchanged from prev.

**Cruxes.** Earlier: an IND or trial start with a space-made formulation; a passed ZBLAN quality
verification plus a paid supply contract; any paid offtake of a space-made semiconductor crystal;
confirming whether historical NBS SRM 1960 (Shuttle-made microspheres sold from 1985, not in the
ledger) meets "beyond novelty" (a gather request, which could even make it achieved). Later: Varda
pivots to reentry services only; ZBLAN verification fails; no trial start reported by end-2027.

## milestone:the-homestead

**Quantity and gap.** A permanently crewed base on the Moon or Mars that has operated for 1+ year
using local resources. Measured as a chain, with gaps:
- (1) a crewed landing: none since 1972;
- (2) a continuously crewed surface habitat: none funded;
- (3) ISRU in service: 4 crew x 0.84 kg O2/day = 3.36 kg/day = 140 g/h, against MOXIE's best
  12 g/h (2023-09-06-moxie-completes-mission-rv), a gap of 11.7x. Lunar MRE oxygen exists only in
  a vacuum chamber on simulant (-rv). The PRIME-1 drill worked, but IM-2 tipped over (-rv). VIPER
  was cancelled in 2024, then revived with a late-2027 launch (-rv);
- (4) one year of operation, a structural floor.
Life-support precursors: Tiangong 100% O2 regeneration (-rv); ISS 98% water recovery (verified);
ISS Sabatier ~47% O2 recovery from CO2 (-rv); ISS food 100% resupplied (legacy); Lunar Palace
closed atmosphere, water and food for 4 crew for a year on the ground (-rv).

**Data points (dates).**
- Artemis III: official NET mid-2027 (legacy, Mar 2025). By Oct 2025 SpaceX's internal earliest
  was Sep 2028, needing up to 12 refuellings (-rv), and NASA reopened HLS to rival bids (-rv).
- China: crewed landing ~2030 (-rv, Apr 2025); ILRS "basic model" by 2035 (projection, -rv; not
  stated as crewed).
- Policy: the Dec 2025 executive order sets a 2028 Moon return (-rv); the FY2026 budget request
  proposes NASA cuts and SLS termination (legacy). Gateway PPE+HALO launch ahead of Artemis IV (-rv).
- Lift is not binding: a base needs ~10^2-10^3 t/yr against 3,194 t/yr total in 2025, growing
  +44.7%/yr (payload-mass-to-orbit).

**Model and why.** There is no series for the quantity itself. It is a chain of structural floors
fed by discounted proponent landing dates, with a reference-class landing-to-base gap. The two
national programmes compete (the earliest wins), each with an abandonment risk. Script
homestead.py (N=400k, seed 11):
- US landing: 2025.8 + 2.9 yr x lognormal discount (median 1.8, 80% 1.3-2.8). Artemis III has
  slipped repeatedly (official 2027 -> 2028+), hence a deep-tech discount at the upper middle of
  1.5-3x.
- China landing: 2025.3 + 4.7 yr x lognormal (median 1.15, 80% 1.0-1.5). China's programme has
  mostly held its dates.
- Combined first landing: median 2031, p10 2030.
- Landing -> permanently crewed base with ISRU: lognormal(median 9 yr, 80% 5-16). Reference
  class: Tiangong-1 (2011) to a continuously crewed Tiangong (2022) took 11 yr; Salyut 1 (1971) to
  long-duration Mir (1986-89) took 15-18 yr; ISS took 2 yr from first module to continuous crew
  (1998->2000), but on decades of station heritage; the ILRS plan (landing 2030 -> basic model
  2035) is 5 yr and robotic-first. A surface base with ISRU is harder than a LEO station, so I use
  9 yr (prev used 7.5). Then the 1-yr operation floor.
- Abandonment: US 0.30, China 0.25 (precedents: Apollo, Constellation 2010, the proposed FY2026
  SLS cut; only landings are funded, no base). If both abandon, a 0.5 chance of a later programme
  starting U(2045, 2070) + lognormal(12 yr, 80% 7-20) + 1.
- Common mode: P(infeasible this century) 0.10 (food closure only on the ground, ISRU at g/h).

**Results.**
- Base case: p_achieved 0.866; unconditional p10 2037, p50 2041, p90 null. If achieved:
  2037/2040/2048. P(by 2036) 0.075, P(by 2040) 0.444.
- Sensitivity:
  - gap 7.5 yr: p50 2040 (P by 2040 0.576)
  - gap 12 yr: p50 2044
  - common mode 0.20: p_achieved 0.77, p50 2042
  - abandonment 0.45/0.40: p_achieved 0.82, p50 2042
  Every variant has a median of 2040 or later, so the status is red in all of them.

**Cross-checks.**
- Proponents: ILRS basic model 2035 x1.5 = ~2040 (and it is not explicitly crewed).
- NASA/US: a 2028 Moon goal with no base funded.
- Previous ETA (2026-10-13, v4): p_achieved 0.89, 2035/2039/null. Mine is 2037/2041, with the
  2-yr later median due to the 9-yr gap (vs 7.5) and a common mode of 0.10 (vs 0.08). Both readings
  are well past 10 years.

**Status.** p50 2041 > 2036, so red ("Distant"). Unchanged.

**Cruxes.** Earlier: an orbital propellant-transfer demo in 2026-27; a crewed landing (Artemis III
or China) by 2030; a funded surface-habitat contract; ISRU oxygen at >= 100 g/h; VIPER (late 2027)
confirming accessible ice. Later: Artemis III re-dated past 2030; SLS/Artemis cancelled without a
replacement; ILRS crewed phase re-dated past 2040.
