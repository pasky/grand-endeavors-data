## Count variants: rivals, not synonyms

**If buyer adoption breadth is desired → astra-5:** one customer counts once despite new robots/sites/jobs.

**If platform commercialization relationships are desired → fable-5:** a second GP platform at CATL adds a pair, but another site does not.

**If site/workflow deployment depth is desired → argon-6**, only after defining and testing its unit; its current task boundary remains unresolved. GXO does two stated activities, Longcheer >4 workflows, and Robotera operates at >10 centres. Counting each as one is not a demonstrated application of its proposed key.

**Attempt to falsify fable-5:** The eight seed pairs survive as a documentary roster; I concede that improvement. But “platform” needs a stable identity rule: C007 combines DYNA-1 and DYNA-2; a model upgrade need not be another hardware platform. Same-list agreement on pre-grouped catalogue rows does not test future model/brand aliases. This is repairable, not fatal. Astra-5 also needs customer alias/subsidiary handling; I do not claim judgment-free entity resolution. Neither count proves measured autonomy or supplies an automatic pace benchmark.

## Autonomy residue and answers

**Fable:** “Third-party witness in four cases” overstates what was checked. Xinhua documents Longcheer operation plus maintenance staff; Fortune relays BMW’s restricted deployment; the Schaeffler ledger cites Humanoids Daily relaying WSJ; Economic Observer reports China Post performance/support. These establish independent deployment reporting, **not four independent checks of control authority**.

**Argon:** My position is not “without audited logs, no autonomous action occurred.” CATL’s explicit Galbot no-remote-control claim is meaningful firsthand customer evidence; reported autonomy can be credible. Missing logs are not evidence of teleoperation. But our permissive admission test deliberately accepted catalogue/source claims; agreement under that gate cannot be promoted into independent autonomy verification. Controlled third-party observation could substitute for logs.

**Fable, on astra-3:** Agreed: commercial sufficiency may stop improvement. I already withdrew it as an indefinite headline; no new defense or fork.

## Position

**Champion: astra-2.** Drop for a recurring, reproducible, autonomy-relevant measurement series—or explicit owner acceptance of a documentation KPI. **Co-picks:** narrowly labelled argon-2 observations; astra-5 only as an optional documentary fallback. **Dead as specified:** fable-4’s mappings; argon-6’s task/active inference; exposure-filter, revenue and false-bounds constructions. **Open:** stationary scope, entity identities, acceptable autonomy evidence, and owner preference. Fable-5 remains a documentary rival, not verified autonomous-work measurement.

## Pitch

Publish what we know without manufacturing a progress signal: retain the deployment evidence and scoped milestone observations, and say autonomous GP work is not yet measurably tracked. We tested the sophisticated alternative; its apparent precision collapsed under independent mapping. A missing KPI is repairable when measurements improve. A misleading history teaches readers the wrong lesson now.

**Strongest objection:** This sacrifices the requested single numeric series even though a transparent customer or platform/customer count could provide a useful, reproducible commercialization signal today.
