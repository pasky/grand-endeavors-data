**Key ruling — sustained:** Fable’s patched **(vendor, customer organization)** key survives my platform-generation objection; Argon’s retained **(GP platform, customer)** wording does not, because DYNA-1/2 or Digit 4/5 requires an additional identity rule. The merger must explicitly adopt the vendor key. Corporate aliases/mergers still need normalization.

**Astra-5 saturation — sustained as a limitation:** A second supplier or job at CATL adds no new customer, so it should add zero to a customer-adoption count. This is deliberate scope, not a calculation error; it makes astra-5 unsuitable as a comprehensive technology-progress index. Fable-5 likewise misses a second job/site won by the *same* vendor/customer pair. Neither should claim otherwise.

**Autonomy objection:** Yes, it applies equally to astra-5 and fable-5. Both are acceptable **documentary fallbacks labelled autonomy-as-reported**, not my preferred measured-autonomy KPI. I do not require audited logs before believing any autonomous work occurs. Argon also overstates the Blue Collar criterion: framework.yaml requires a **disclosed average intervention rate**, not specifically third-party-audited logs.
