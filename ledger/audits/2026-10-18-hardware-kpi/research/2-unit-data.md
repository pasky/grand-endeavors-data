# Humanoid and general-purpose mobile-manipulator unit data (2023–H1 2026)

**Research cut-off: 17 October 2026.** This is a source audit, not a harmonized census. “Humanoid” is not consistently defined: sources may include bipedal humanoids, wheeled humanoids, semi-humanoids, entertainment robots, or a vendor's broader embodied-robot portfolio. Shipment often means maker-reported shipments, not end-customer acceptance, operation, or productive autonomous labor. Figures below preserve the source's wording and scope; estimates are not interchangeable.

## Findings at a glance

* No dependable, comparable annual series for 2023–26 emerged. The 2025 global estimate ranges from **about 13,000 (Omdia)** to **over 20,000 produced (Interact Analysis)** and **over 18,000 shipped (IDC)**. These are not necessarily contradictory: production differs from shipments, and category/vendor coverage may differ. They nevertheless show material uncertainty—at least **7,000 units / ~54%** from Omdia's 13k baseline to Interact's >20k; IDC is ~38% above Omdia.
* For **H1 2026**, available claims range from **19,100 shipped (SAG)** to **nearly 25,000 shipped (IDC, reported secondarily)**. That is a **5,900-unit, ~31% spread** between the 19.1k estimate and the 25k upper approximation, before examining definitions. Annual forecasts are much higher: **>50,000 (TrendForce)** and **90,000 (BofA)**; a claim of **750,000 by 2030 (IDC)** is a forecast, not observed data.
* IDC provides the most useful end-use split found: **>85% of 2025 deployments** went to performances, education, data collection, and guided tours—demonstration, interaction, or validation—rather than clearly productive commercial work. Thus its 18k total implies **<15% (under roughly 2,700 units)** outside those categories, but that residual is not proven to be paid productive units. IDC says manufacturing/logistics pilots exist; it does not give a count of robots doing paid work.
* Best company-level checks include AgiBot's **5,168 2025 annual shipments** (company announcement, citing Omdia) and Unitree's **>5,500** 2025 humanoid shipments in its 2026 IPO prospectus (primary document, but prospectus figure located via secondary coverage; official investor-relations portal linked below). UBTECH reported **1,079 cumulative full-size humanoid sales** in 2025, a distinct metric—not annual shipments. Order values, capacity, factory milestones, and “in operation” marketing claims do not establish units productively working.
* There is no useful public 2023 global baseline located, and 2024 figures are sparse/definition-sensitive. Annual shipments are not presently a trustworthy KPI series. An audited, independently verified “installed in paid productive work” count would be closer to the intended outcome but does not exist as a public recurring series.

### Scope distinction: general-purpose mobile manipulators

This search found abundant forecasts and shipment estimates for **humanoids**, but no credible standalone recurring global series for general-purpose mobile manipulators (mobile base plus manipulators, potentially non-humanoid) that excludes conventional AMRs, specialized warehouse systems, and purpose-built industrial arms. “Embodied intelligent robot” reports can include wheeled humanoids and other forms, but public summaries do not give a consistently separable count. Adding AGVs/AMRs, cobots, or all service robots would change the object being measured and overwhelm the small humanoid series. Any future series would need a tight hardware/category definition and explicit vendor/product roster.

## End use: demos versus productive paid work

The clearest split is IDC's April 2026 account of 2025: **more than 85%** of deployments were in performances, education, data collection, and guided-tour services, mainly demonstrations, interaction, and technology validation. The text separately says early manufacturing/logistics pilots have emerged and describes industrial adoption accelerating; it does **not** quantify factory/logistics units, paid customer deployments, hours worked, or autonomous task completion. The category “guided tour service” may itself be paid, but is not equivalent to replacing productive labor. IDC therefore supports only an upper bound: <15% of its >18,000 total (roughly <2,700 units) were outside the listed demo/education/data collection/guided-tour categories; it does not establish that this remainder worked productively.

Interact Analysis' public summary says most 2025 production was used for non-productive purposes, but gives no audited end-use split in the accessible page. AgiBot/Unitree shipments likely include education, research, entertainment, demonstrations, developer/research use, and commercial pilots, but public shipment totals do not segment destination or labor output. UBTECH's Walker S deployments and company case studies show factory trials, but orders and trial videos do not establish autonomous production hours or the number of robots in paid routine service. For Tesla/Figure/Agility and peers, public factory trials likewise have no sufficiently robust units × autonomous operating hours accounting.

**Verified productive-work unit estimate:** none found. No source located reported a global count of units simultaneously (a) delivered, (b) doing paid operational work for an external customer, (c) autonomous rather than teleoperated or heavily supervised, and (d) routinely productive rather than a trial/demo. Numbers commonly called “commercial deployment” do not verify all four conditions.

## How much do estimates disagree?

* **Full-year 2025:** Omdia ~13k, IDC >18k, Interact Analysis >20k produced. Comparing the quoted thresholds, IDC is at least ~38% above Omdia; Interact's production figure is at least ~54% above Omdia. Omdia and Interact differ by at least 7,000 units, but production-versus-shipment and taxonomy differences prevent treating this as pure counting error. The same year has company totals that do not simply reconcile to Omdia: AgiBot 5,168 + Unitree >5,500 already exceed 10,668, leaving only roughly 2.3k of Omdia's ~13k for all other vendors. This is plausible if Omdia's totals/rounding and product scope match, but not independently audited.
* **H1 2026:** SAG ~19.1k versus IDC nearly 25k: approximate spread 5.9k / 31% of the lower count. It is not clear whether their included product classes and shipment cutoffs are identical.
* **Forecasts for 2026:** TrendForce >50k versus BofA 90k, a gap of at least 40k; BofA is at least 80% higher than the TrendForce threshold. These are forecasts from different methodologies, not actuals. The IDC 2030 forecast changed relative to earlier 2030 projection history (Omdia's 2024 projection was 38k in 2030; IDC's 2026 forecast is >510k), illustrating forecast instability rather than realized growth.

## KPI assessment

**Annual shipments: not yet a trustworthy KPI.** There is no stable historical series, global unit definition, disclosed audit trail, or consistent treatment of demo/research/entertainment stock, exports, returns, and shipments versus recognized sales. The 2025 same-year spread is large, available company totals are selectively disclosed, and shipment volume rewards low-cost demo units as much as useful autonomous labor. 2023–24 history is especially poor; the series is only beginning to be retrospectively estimated.

**Installed base “in commercial work”: closer, but currently weaker in practice.** No public recurring, independently verified global count was located, and a unit count still fails to distinguish autonomous operation from teleoperation, human babysitting, low-utilization pilots, or brief demonstrations. It would need a protocol: named roster and definition; paid external customer; routinely productive task; hours/uptime and human interventions per operating hour; autonomy level; and independent/customer confirmation. Better still, an outcome series might measure autonomous productive robot-hours or verified human labor-hours displaced, but such data are not currently standardized/public.

**History length:** potentially retrospectively estimable for shipments only from about 2024/2025, with major uncertainty; not a defensible 2023–26 comparable time series. No meaningful installed productive-base series or history length exists in public sources. Recommend not selecting either as the Robots/HARDWARE KPI yet. Track shipments and disclosed customer deployments as contextual indicators, explicitly labeled estimates, while pursuing an autonomy-adjusted productive-hours measure.
