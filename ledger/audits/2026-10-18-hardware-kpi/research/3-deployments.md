# Robots/HARDWARE KPI research: commercial deployment disclosures

**Cut-off:** 17 October 2026 (as specified for this research). **Scope:** reported customer-site deployment of general-purpose humanoid/wheeled humanoid/mobile-manipulator robots, 2024–cut-off. “Paid” means a publicly documented commercial/revenue agreement, not merely a purchase order. A named customer and industrial setting alone do not prove payment. Company self-report is not treated as independent verification. “Not disclosed” means no figure located in cited public material, not zero.

## Findings at a glance

Commercial work is real, but public reporting is overwhelmingly qualitative. Only two sources in this review contain enough operands to calculate even nominal robot-hours × throughput: Figure/BMW (hours and parts, but not robot count or realized hourly output) and AGIBOT/Longcheer (output/hour and output/shift, but not fleet size or robot-hours; 140+ continuous hours is reported). **Strictly, zero deployments disclose all three of robot count, hours and throughput for a reproducible fleet-work total.** At most 2 of 12 deployments in the inventory disclose enough partial operands for a bounded, non-comparable estimate; 0/12 provides the complete set. Autonomy/intervention evidence is also sparse: Figure defines an intervention KPI and target but does not publish its realized value; Longcheer claims “minimal human intervention” but gives no count/rate. No reviewed deployment publishes a teleoperation rate or quantified babysitter staffing.

Thus a bottom-up public-data KPI for autonomous paid robot-hours, human-labor-equivalents, or aggregate fleet output is not feasible today. The best public “largest verified” claim would still be a fragile disclosure leaderboard, not a stable time series. Disclosure itself grows, but the disclosed total of work cannot be reliably calculated or shown to be growing. A future KPI needs a standardized, audited disclosure protocol.

### What was and was not disclosed

The inventory has **12 rows**, counting the most substantial named deployment leads plus excluded named candidates, and **7 supported commercial/customer-site cases** only if “commercial/customer-site” is read broadly to include pilots, training and announcements (BMW, GXO, Mercedes, BYD, NIO/Zeekr/FAW-VW, Longcheer, CATL). On the strict “paid live work” test, the public evidence supports **GXO** as an explicit paid/RaaS deployment; Figure/BMW is clearly real production deployment but its payment is not public; Longcheer is live production but commercial payment is not public. The rest are pilot, training, or insufficiently verified. Do not present them as seven known paid fleets.

Metric availability in this intentionally conservative inventory:

- Complete `robot count × hours × throughput`: **0/12 (0%)**. No reviewed case supplies all three as fleet-level, attributable actuals.
- Some usable work operands: **2/12 (17%)**—Figure/BMW publishes hours plus parts (count and throughput rate missing); AGIBOT/Longcheer publishes throughput and cumulative operating time but not fleet count, with ambiguity about per-robot versus system figures.
- Any meaningful autonomy/intervention disclosure: **2/12 (17%)**—Figure defines intervention/success targets but not achieved values; AGIBOT claims autonomy and minimal intervention but no intervention count. Zero publish teleoperation frequency/rate or quantified human supervisor staffing.
- Paid status explicit: **1/12 (8%)**—GXO’s multi-year RaaS, revenue-generating agreement. This is a minimum count based on evidence available, not the true share of deployments that are paid.

These are **disclosure rates in this curated, non-random set**, not industry prevalence estimates. Several source pages combine multiple customers, and counting each as a separate row risks double counting the same platform or pilot. Missing data are not interpreted as zero work or low autonomy.

## KPI feasibility and trend

A KPI such as **autonomous robot-hours in paid work** is conceptually attractive: count only productive hours during which a robot performs the task without teleoperation, then subtract/flag human interventions; require customer validation and clear task boundaries. But current disclosures lack fleet count, productive hours, autonomy denominator, human support and consistent accounting. Vendor-reported “operational hours” may include idle/testing time; 24/7 claims may describe capability, not observed operations. Without audit trails, a single operator supervising a fleet and one-to-one teleoperation can both be marketed as autonomous operation.

**FTE-equivalents** are even less feasible: sites rarely publish matched human baseline, task mix, utilization, or net labor displaced/relocated. Figure’s 30,000 vehicles “contributed to production” is not a human-equivalent measure. The 310 units/hour Longcheer metric could be meaningful if its unit is a cell/fleet and comparable baseline were disclosed, but neither is currently clear.

**Largest verified autonomous deployment** is the least data-hungry alternative, but still fails the central autonomy test: public accounts do not expose control mode, teleoperation, staffing, intervention rates, or audited fleet size. A commercial deal alone measures procurement/PR rather than work performed.

The only defensible trend statement is that named customer pilots and live-use announcements broadened from the 2024 GXO and Mercedes deals to Figure/BMW production reporting in 2025 and Longcheer/CATL reports in 2025–26. **No numeric disclosed-work growth rate can be calculated.** Publicly available headline numbers are isolated, non-comparable snapshots, not an accumulating series. A trustworthy KPI would need a reporting standard: fleet/site IDs; paid status; deployed and productive units; clocked task-hours; task output and errors; downtime; interventions and teleop minutes; human supervisors per robot; and customer sign-off, with annual historical restatement.

## Caveats

This is a disclosure audit, not a census. Exact paid status is often confidential; company announcements are interested-party claims; Chinese-language source discovery and translated reports may omit context. Details not expressly supported above remain unverified. No fleet-wide numbers have been inferred from contracts, order books, announced production capacity, demos, or promised future rollouts.
