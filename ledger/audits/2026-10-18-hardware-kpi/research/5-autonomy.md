# Measuring autonomy in general-purpose physical robots

**Research cut-off: 17 October 2026.** This memo asks whether deployed/demonstrated humanoids are genuinely autonomous, what data could establish that, and whether a recurring public series exists. “Autonomy” here means task execution without a person supplying action-level control—not merely a robot being powered, moving, or producing useful output. Remote observation, high-level task assignment, and exceptional safety stops should be recorded separately from continuous teleoperation and human recovery.

## Executive finding

**No trustworthy recurring industry-wide series measures autonomy of deployed general-purpose robots.** Public sources offer striking but non-comparable facts: acknowledged teleoperation/supervision modes, company-reported runtime/task counts, and occasional benchmark scores. They do not report a consistent denominator of robot-hours or task attempts, nor independently auditable intervention logs. An FTE-equivalent/output figure therefore cannot establish autonomous labor substitution.

The best available near-term approach is a **standardized, independently audited autonomy ledger**: autonomous productive robot-hours divided by total scheduled robot-hours, with task output/quality, interventions and remote-control time published alongside it. For a public headline KPI, report the audited autonomous fraction of productive work in the largest qualifying deployment, rather than a single endurance record. A recurring deployment series does not yet exist; creating one would require common definitions and compelled or contractual reporting.

## (a) Teleoperation: known prevalence, unknown prevalence rate

There is no credible estimate of the percentage of humanoid deployments or demos that involve teleoperation. Firms do not publish a fleet-wide share, and available anecdotes are selected examples—not a representative sample. “Autonomous” also spans materially different modes: autonomous execution with remote oversight, occasional teleoperation after failure, scheduled expert guidance, and continuous human joystick/VR control. These must not be combined.

- **Tesla Optimus, October 2024:** Bloomberg reported that humans remotely operated some capabilities of Optimus prototypes at Tesla’s *We, Robot* event. TechCrunch reported the allegations and noted that Tesla had not explained exactly which actions were remote-controlled. This establishes that some event interactions were not autonomous; it does **not** establish that every Optimus demo or task was teleoperated. No public share or machine-readable event log was provided. [Bloomberg, 14 Oct 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-10-14/tesla-s-optimus-robots-were-remotely-operated-at-cybercab-event); [TechCrunch, 14 Oct 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/14/tesla-optimus-bots-were-controlled-by-humans-during-the-we-robot-event/).
- **1X NEO:** 1X’s current product page explicitly says NEO works autonomously by default, but an “Expert” can remotely guide unfamiliar chores at scheduled times; the page also describes piloting NEO remotely through its app/VR device. This is a disclosed hybrid operating model, but there is no published utilization rate for Expert Mode, the number of Experts per robot, or the share of completed chores that require it. [1X NEO product page](https://www.1x.tech/neo) (accessed 17 Oct 2026; page presents itself as a current product page, not a dated report).
- **Figure / BMW:** Figure’s retrospective on its 11-month Figure 02 deployment describes daily production-line work and reports 1,250+ runtime hours, 90,000+ parts loaded, and a zero-human-intervention-per-shift *goal*. It specifies that interventions were logged, but the public summary gives no observed intervention count, teleoperation hours, staffing ratio, or autonomy share. Its published results are useful evidence of real deployment, but not a disclosed autonomy rate. [Figure, 19 Nov 2025](https://www.figure.ai/news/production-at-bmw).
- **Chinese factory demonstrations:** AGIBOT’s June 2026 Longcheer release reports multiple robots working a live tablet-inspection line during a six-day livestream: **64+ hours**, **64,828 production-line tasks**, **99.99% task success**, and **17,625 cumulative line output**. These are company-reported totals. The release does not provide the robot-hour denominator by unit, intervention/teleoperation log, operator-to-robot ratio, or independent audit methodology; it therefore cannot be read as proof of 64 hours of uninterrupted autonomous operation. [AGIBOT, 29 Jun 2026](https://www.agibot.com/article/231/detail/83.html). The primary release’s own wording says “multiple” robots and does not clearly define whether the 64 hours are aggregate or wall-clock operation; **do not infer a per-robot 64-hour run**.
- **Chinese / broader remote-operator labor models:** Public sources reviewed did not establish a verifiable, sector-wide operator:robot ratio for humanoids, or teleoperation prevalence in Chinese commercial deployments. Remote-control research and recruitment/“robotics data collection” work are evidence that human-in-the-loop labor exists, not a defensible estimate of the fraction of deployed tasks it performs. Claims of “one operator supervising N robots” need a definition of active control, observation, task assignment, recovery, and concurrent workload before comparison.

**Bottom line on prevalence:** verified examples establish a nonzero and sometimes intentionally productized role for human remote control. They do not support a numeric population estimate. A survey of marketing videos, press releases, or job postings would be selection-biased and should not be presented as teleop share.

## (b) Interventions, disengagements, safety and reliability

### California DMV as precedent—and warning

California’s autonomous-vehicle testing program has collected annual disengagement reports for years. DMV defines a disengagement as a situation in which the autonomous system is disengaged and a human driver takes control, including failures or situations requiring test-driver intervention. Reports include miles and disengagement counts, allowing a crude rate per mile. The latest DMV release at this research cut-off describes the **1 Dec 2024–30 Nov 2025** reporting period and says permit holders logged more than **9 million test miles**; DMV continues to publish annual reports. [California DMV AV program and reports](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-industry-services/autonomous-vehicles/); [DMV, 20 Feb 2026 release](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/news-and-media/autonomous-vehicle-permit-holders-in-california-logged-more-than-9-million-test-miles-between-december-1-2024-and-november-30-2025/).

This is a useful *institutional* precedent (standardized event definition, recurring cadence, exposure denominator, regulator-hosted disclosure), not a clean autonomy KPI. Companies decide whether and when to disengage; testing routes, weather, system boundaries, and reporting classifications vary; disengagements are not equivalent in severity; and more conservative reporting can perversely look worse. Rate per mile also misses task complexity and total duration. An academic analysis of **159,840** reports from Sep 2014–Nov 2018 found 75% categorized as “planned” disengagements, illustrating how much classification matters. [Skokan et al., 2025, *Vehicles*](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-8921/7/2/32). DMV reports provide history, but changed reporting requirements and incomparable operational design domains complicate long-run rankings.

### Robot intervention metrics available today

- Figure’s BMW case is unusually concrete: its disclosed deployment KPI was “Interventions”—human pauses or resets—with a target of **zero per shift**. The published summary does not report actual intervention counts, though it says interventions were logged. [Figure deployment report](https://www.figure.ai/news/production-at-bmw).
- Covariant has advertised operation without human intervention **more than 99.9% of the time**, and its published putwall material cites less than **0.1%** of orders requiring intervention. These are vendor-reported legacy warehouse examples, not humanoid statistics and not an independently standardized series; the denominators and definition of intervention are not fully specified in the cited public material. [Covariant company site](https://covariant.ai/) (company claims; page content may change); [Covariant putwall video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=24zGP15OEAY).
- Agility’s site claims Digit has **65,000 hours of real production experience / operation** as of its September 2026 Digit 5 material. That is significant accumulated exposure, but the public claim does not state autonomous fraction, number of units, productive fraction, intervention rate, or whether all hours are customer-production hours. Do not treat it as 65,000 autonomous hours. [Agility Digit 5, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.agilityrobotics.com/content/agility-unveils-digit-5-humanoid-robot-built-for-cooperatively-safe-work-at-scale); [Agility solutions](https://www.agilityrobotics.com/solutions).
- Among Agility, Figure, AgiBot, Dyna, Ocado and Amazon sources reviewed, only Figure clearly stated an intervention metric in a humanoid field deployment; none supplied a comparable public humanoid intervention time series or a verified operator:robot ratio. Amazon’s large warehouse robot fleet and operational metrics mostly concern purpose-built systems, outside the KPI’s defined scope; they cannot be used as proxy evidence for general-purpose autonomous humanoids. [Amazon overview, 9 Oct 2024](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/operations/amazon-robotics-robots-fulfillment-center).

### Safety certification is not autonomy verification

Agility announced that Digit passed an **OSHA-recognized NRTL safety field inspection** at a customer fulfillment site in November 2025. This is meaningful evidence about safety assessment at that site, not the robot’s task autonomy or intervention frequency. [Agility, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.agilityrobotics.com/content/beyond-the-hype). UL’s service information describes certification of commercial robots to **UL 3300**; OSHA recognition of an NRTL and a product’s certification address safety requirements, not autonomous performance. [UL robot services](https://www.ul.com/services/consumer-and-commercial-robots); [OSHA NRTL program](https://www.osha.gov/nationally-recognized-testing-laboratory-program).

**ISO 25785-1** is being developed for safety requirements for industrial mobile robots with actively controlled stability. The ISO page identifies it as a committee draft (CD) at the time of source review; it is a safety standard in development, not a passed autonomy test or an existing public performance dataset. [ISO/CD 25785-1](https://www.iso.org/standard/91469.html); [Agility glossary](https://www.agilityrobotics.com/glossary). Safety certification can be a prerequisite for deployments but cannot substitute for logged operational autonomy.

## (c) Real-world benchmarks: useful experiments, no KPI series yet

- **RoboArena:** A 2025 research proposal for distributed, real-robot evaluation of generalist policies. Human participants execute/evaluate policies on robots and compare outcomes; the paper reports **600+ pairwise real-robot comparisons**. This is promising scalable *research evaluation*, but it is not an autonomous shift benchmark, does not score teleoperation/intervention burden as a deployment outcome, and does not yet provide a stable annual public leaderboard series. [RoboArena paper, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18123v1); [project site](https://robo-arena.github.io/).
- **BEHAVIOR Challenge:** Stanford’s 2025 challenge evaluated 50 everyday household tasks, seeking long-horizon reasoning, locomotion, and dexterous manipulation. It is valuable real-world-oriented benchmark design, but results have been run as a challenge/edition rather than a long historical physical-autonomy series. The 2026 challenge is a further edition. Important limitation: distinguish actual physical-robot results from benchmark simulation or virtual evaluations; the BEHAVIOR-1K benchmark itself is simulation-based. [2025 challenge archive](https://behavior.stanford.edu/challenge/archive/2025/index.html); [2026 challenge](https://behavior.stanford.edu/challenge/index.html); [Stanford HAI overview, 22 Sep 2025](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/behavior-challenge-charts-the-way-forward-for-domestic-robotics); [BEHAVIOR-1K](https://behavior.stanford.edu/index.html).
- **AgiBot World Challenge:** The 2025 inaugural challenge involved real-world robot task scenarios and an open challenge format; a 2026 edition advertises 10 real-world scenarios and ranks by task success. It creates edition-to-edition potential, but challenge participants and tasks are not representative of field deployment; the vendor is also the challenge organizer. Treat as benchmark evidence, not an independent population measure. [2025 challenge](https://agibot-world.com/challenge2025); [2026 challenge](https://agibot-world.com/challenge2026); [AGIBOT announcement, 2026](https://www.agibot.com/article/231/detail/45.html).
- **RoboCup@Home:** Since 2006, recurring competition using tasks in realistic but non-standardized home environments. Annual rules/results give historical competition records; variable tasks, staging, and a small competitive field limit longitudinal comparability. It demonstrates broad service-robot capabilities, but is not a standardized time-series of autonomous task success at human productivity. [League site](https://athome.robocup.org/); [2025 call for participation](https://athome.robocup.org/2025-cfp1-all/).
- **World Humanoid Robot Games, Beijing:** The 2025 and 2026 events provide recurring public competitions. Many events (sports, performance, obstacle/task events) measure athletic or contest ability, not flexible labor. In 2026, some events specifically required autonomy (e.g. 100m running), but a race is not general-purpose task autonomy. Robotics rules and external reporting must specify which events permit remote control and what “autonomous” means. [Official event page](https://www.robocup.org/events/89); [Reuters, 23 Aug 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/robots-can-outrun-humans-can-they-plug-cable-2026-08-23/).
- **ANA Avatar XPRIZE:** Finals judged telepresence/remote avatar performance. This is intentionally human-operated embodiment, useful as a reminder that impressive physical task completion can be powered by a remote human; it is not an autonomous robot benchmark. [XPRIZE competition](https://www.xprize.org/competitions/avatar); [winners](https://www.xprize.org/news/ana-avatar-xprize-winners).
- **DARPA:** DARPA challenges have delivered one-off, well-defined tests (e.g. the 2015 Robotics Challenge) and strong historical value, but no current recurring open-field KPI for general-purpose physical autonomy was found. They should not be presented as a current annual series.
- **Foundation-model evaluation reports:** Google DeepMind’s 2025 Gemini Robotics paper reports physical-robot evaluations and transfer/generalization tests, and subsequent model pages report task success measures. These are useful individual studies, often with small task sets and controlled test sites. Physical Intelligence’s π0/π0.5 publications likewise report robot tasks and generalization, but no common, continuously maintained cross-company public report series exists. Model-specific success percentages are not directly comparable without common robot, task, environment, trial count, reset, and intervention protocols. [Gemini Robotics paper, Mar 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20020v1); [DeepMind robotics blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-brings-whole-body-intelligence-to-robots/); [Physical Intelligence π0.5 overview, 22 Apr 2025](https://www.physicalintelligence.company/blog/pi05); [paper PDF](https://www.physicalintelligence.company/download/pi05.pdf).
- **“Robot time horizon”:** METR’s human-task time horizon is a useful analogy, but it measures software-task duration. No established equivalent recurring metric was found for robots completing a task autonomously at human-speed productivity in real, changing environments. A task-duration measure alone risks rewarding a robot for slowly remaining active; pair duration with quality/output and autonomy fraction.

### What a useful physical benchmark must specify

A valid benchmark needs frozen task definitions, randomized and genuinely unseen scenes, a published human reference throughput, fixed success/quality thresholds, repeated trials, and video/telemetry audit. Report (1) task success and quality, (2) elapsed and productive time, (3) autonomous fraction, (4) remote-control minutes and active operator interventions, (5) safety stops and recoveries, and (6) robot failures/downtime. Release task-level results and uncertainty, not just a single aggregate percentage. Held-out environments and independent administration reduce demo selection and benchmark overfitting.

## (d) Continuous-operation records and independent verification

- **AGIBOT / Longcheer, June 2026:** Company says multiple robots accumulated 64+ hours of operation over six days and completed 64,828 tasks with 99.99% success. The livestream is a stronger transparency signal than an edited demo, but the release is the company’s own account; no independent audit or continuous third-party log was located. Aggregate hours across multiple machines are not a single-robot endurance record. It reports production tasks/output, but not autonomous time, operator intervention, or per-robot allocation. [AGIBOT, 29 Jun 2026](https://www.agibot.com/article/231/detail/83.html).
- **Figure “200-hour” sorting/stream:** Search results and reposts in 2026 describe a 200-hour Figure package-sorting test, but I could not verify an authoritative Figure primary source providing a detailed 200-hour autonomous-operation record, nor identify a third-party audit with raw telemetry and intervention logs. **Do not treat the 200-hour claim as verified.** Figure’s independently viewable official BMW retrospective instead reports 1,250+ runtime hours over an 11-month deployment, 10-hour weekday shifts, and 90,000+ parts loaded—still company-reported, and explicitly missing actual intervention counts in the public report. [Figure BMW report](https://www.figure.ai/news/production-at-bmw).
- **Agility 65,000 hours:** A primary company claim for accumulated production experience/operation as of Sep 2026; not a record of a continuous run and not disclosed as autonomous hours. No independent audit or public unit-level intervention ledger located. [Agility Digit 5 announcement](https://www.agilityrobotics.com/content/agility-unveils-digit-5-humanoid-robot-built-for-cooperatively-safe-work-at-scale).
- **Verification verdict:** Livestreaming verifies that activity was shown for a period, not that the robot was autonomous throughout, that the stream was complete/uninterrupted, or that all shown work passed quality acceptance. Strong verification would publish tamper-evident robot telemetry, continuous video with gaps annotated, per-unit clocks, operator-console logs, task acceptance records, downtime, and an independent auditor’s sampling/report.

## KPI proposals and rating

Ratings are relative to the present data landscape: **availability** means existing public data; **history** means a usable recurring comparable series; **gameability** means ease of presenting a flattering number; **cadence** means realistic recurring publication.

| Candidate KPI | Data availability | History | Gameability | Cadence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Autonomous productive-hours share in largest qualifying deployment** = task-producing hours without action-level human control ÷ all scheduled robot-hours; publish total output/quality and safety/intervention breakdown | Low today; needs operator/customer ledger | None | Medium unless audited; can cherry-pick site/tasks | Potential quarterly/annual | **Best practical outcome KPI.** Directly addresses labor replacement and autonomy; useful denominator; requires agreed definitions and third-party checks. |
| **Interventions per 1,000 productive robot-hours** at largest disclosed deployment, with remote-control minutes and intervention severity separately reported | Low; Figure logged but did not disclose count | None across general-purpose robots | High: intervention boundaries/classification are manipulable | Potential quarterly | Strong diagnostic, not stand-alone. Count human prompts/recoveries/resets, include all attempted work and publish logs. |
| **Largest independently verified autonomous shift** at ≥80% of matched human throughput, accepted quality, no action-level teleoperation; disclose interventions and operator:robot ratio | Very low | No recurring series | High: site/task selection, threshold, and definition of “shift” | Annual records feasible | Compelling “record,” poor KPI; one exceptional task/site can dominate, no stable historical denominator. |
| **Zero-shot task success in unseen real homes** on fixed standard set (e.g. BEHAVIOR-style tasks), with human baseline and intervention-free success | Low; emerging challenges/papers | Short, fragmented | Medium-high: task selection, resets, cherry-picked scenes | Annual challenge possible | Best research benchmark route for generality; not an operating labor outcome and home tasks difficult to standardize safely. |
| **Longest autonomous task time horizon** at human-normalized throughput and quality | Very low | None | High unless task suite/failure rule fixed | Annual benchmark possible | Useful companion metric, not standalone. Long duration without output/quality and autonomy denominator is misleading. |
| Safety certification / incidents per robot-hour | Some safety certification; sparse exposure data | Low | Medium | Certification event / annual safety reporting | Essential guardrail but **not autonomy**. Track separately. |

### Recommended definition and reporting template

If the owner wants “how much human labor robots have taken over,” keep labor output as the outcome but make its automation claim auditable:

> **Autonomous productive robot-hours** = productive hours in which the robot completes assigned tasks with no human action-level commands, teleoperation, or physical intervention. Report totals, share of scheduled hours, matched human throughput/quality, and intervention/teleoperation minutes per 1,000 productive hours.

Include high-level assignment/monitoring as autonomous only if the operator is not guiding moment-to-moment behavior; record rescue, reset, and exception handling as intervention even if rare. Report human staffing required for deployment/maintenance separately, not hidden as “supervision.” Do not equate unit shipment, runtime, uptime, livestream length, or claimed output with autonomous work.

**Suggested section KPI (once reporting exists):** “Audited autonomous productive hours in the largest disclosed general-purpose robot deployment, per reporting year,” accompanied by autonomy share, human-equivalent accepted output, and intervention rate. Until enough firms report this consistently, publish a cautious evidence tracker with explicit unknowns rather than a nominal numeric series that rewards non-disclosure or PR.

## Source notes and limits

Primary company and regulator sources are used where available; trade press is used for the Tesla event controversy and contextual claims. Company-reported performance is identified as such, never treated as independently verified. We did not locate publicly available robot operation logs, a representative teleoperation survey, an industry-wide intervention reporting rule, an auditable 200-hour Figure primary report, or a recurring cross-vendor autonomous-work series. Search absence is not proof that private data do not exist.
