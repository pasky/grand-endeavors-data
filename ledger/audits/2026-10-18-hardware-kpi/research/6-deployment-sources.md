# Robots deployment KPI: recurring sources and candidates

**Research cut-off: 17 October 2026.** Scope: productive work performed by general-purpose autonomous physical robots (humanoids/mobile manipulators), or the closest measurable proxy (AI-enabled/autonomous mobile robots). “Deployment” means robots actually operating in production, not announced orders, factory capacity, shipments, demonstration videos, or planned deployments. None of the public recurring sources below presently reports a verified aggregate of autonomous GP robot work-hours or work-time share. “Autonomy-aware” means the source distinguishes robot work without continuous human control/intervention, not merely that a machine is called autonomous/AI-powered.

## Bottom line

There is no ready-made deployment KPI. The best near-term route is a **triangulated index**, not a false-precision unit count: (1) a recurring, representative employer module that asks actual operating hours/tasks and human intervention; (2) audited company fleet-hours/units-in-customer-production disclosures; and (3) structured procurement/shipments as a lead indicator, with end-use and autonomy verified. Census ABS/ACES and Eurostat offer useful historical baselines but are mostly conventional-robot measures; they do not isolate GP capability or autonomous labor. Most humanoid market numbers count shipments, with substantial research, education, entertainment, and data-collection demand.

## Ranked five most promising

1. **Purpose-built representative employer survey (Census ABS/BTOS supplement; or a repeat Yotzov/DMP-style panel)** — only route to a population-weighted denominator and actual hours. Add a module asking deployed robot type, use case, work hours, independent run-time, teleoperation/supervision/intervention, paid production vs pilot, and task/output. Existing surveys do not ask those questions; a new module is needed.
2. **Public-company filings with installed base + operations hours** — best verifiability and recurring cadence when disclosed. UBTECH and (as of 2026) Agility provide a foothold, but disclosures conflate deliveries/orders/hours and are incomplete; seek explicit customer-site operating fleet and autonomy logs.
3. **Chinese procurement award notices, normalized and coded** — unusually concrete buyer, quantity, amount, and contract purpose. Track awarded/fulfilled tenders, not notices; classify education/research/venues vs production, and confirm delivery/operation. Fragmented access and procurement is not use.
4. **IFR + analyst shipment/end-use trackers (IDC, Counterpoint, GGII/Omdia, etc.)** — recurring and increasingly granular, useful for bounding market and composition, but shipments are not operating stock or productive autonomous labor. Keep as an ancillary leading indicator only.
5. **Robot telemetry/audited operational hours (Agility and other vendors/customers)** — closest direct outcome metric; potentially highly autonomy-aware, but currently selective, company-controlled, non-representative and definition-sensitive. Build a common audited protocol rather than extrapolate one vendor’s hours.

Rank is usefulness for a national work-share KPI, not current data quality alone. All five require autonomy and production-stage checks.

## 2. China procurement and public tenders

**What can be measured:** notices and award records may show buyer, bid/award price, named robot/model, quantity and stated purpose. Public portals include [China Government Procurement Network](http://www.ccgp.gov.cn/), provincial procurement/bidding portals, and [China Tendering and Bidding Public Service Platform](https://www.cebpubservice.com/). Commercial aggregators/search services include [China Bidding](https://www.chinabidding.com/), and global tender aggregators; coverage, deduplication and full-text access vary. There is no authoritative publicly accessible recurring national humanoid-tender statistical series located.

**Examples / aggregations:** Reuters and other reporting have described Chinese government/PLA humanoid tender trails, including research/training purchases; media reporting on 2026 public procurement projects cites larger commercial/industrial orders. These are individual records, not verified completed deployments. [Example reporting on procurement records (secondary; verify notice-level)](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/326931/20260908/chinas-pla-has-100-procurement-records-combat-humanoids-zero-deployments.htm). Industry outlets and analyst houses (GGII/Gaogong, 36Kr, Securities Times) publish shipment/market estimates and individual order news; I found **no transparent, regularly updated, auditable aggregation** of tenders that consistently reports buyer class, winning bidder, awarded quantity, delivery and actual production use. **Unverified:** specific media claims such as hundreds of units / tens of millions of dollars cited in search results were not corroborated against tender award notices and must not be treated as actual installed robots.

**Specificity / autonomy:** A humanoid named in the tender makes this relatively GP-specific, but broad tender labels can conceal education, research, exhibition, data collection or pilot use. A public tender cannot establish autonomy or operation. Track tender **award + delivery + acceptance + production task**; code buyer sector and purpose separately. Avoid counting bid invitations, framework agreements, proposed awards or procurement budgets as fulfilled units.

**Assessment:** Strong high-resolution pipeline/procurement indicator, weak national denominator and weak proof of work. Access is fragmented and may require Chinese searches, downloads or paid feeds. Procurement is a likely first source to build a recurring newsworthy trend if records are manually validated, but it is not itself the KPI.

## 5. Job-posting and labor-market proxies

**Sources:** Lightcast job-posting datasets/analytics ([Lightcast](https://lightcast.io/)); Indeed Hiring Lab and job search ([Indeed Hiring Lab](https://www.hiringlab.org/)); public company career pages and LinkedIn are complementary. Track mentions of humanoid/robot operator, fleet technician, robot trainer, teleoperator, autonomy supervisor, and deployment engineer by industry, location, employer, deduplicated posting and time.

**What it measures:** hiring demand or the labor required to deploy/maintain/control robots, not robot count or productive hours. A rise in teleoperators could signal constrained autonomy rather than autonomous work; technician roles can reflect pilots or manufacturing capacity. Most job titles are ambiguous (“robotics engineer” often designs robots, not operates a deployed fleet). No public recurring Lightcast/Indeed series specific to humanoid robot operators or teleoperators, and no substantiated latest value located. **GP-specific: weakly, if explicitly named. Autonomy-aware: potentially negative proxy (teleoperation/control jobs), not positive proof.** Proprietary microdata access likely paid; public analysis can be built but needs carefully validated dictionary and employer/site context.

## 6. Safety/regulatory records

- **US OSHA NRTL certification / field labels:** OSHA lists recognized laboratories and their certification scopes, not a national register of workplace robot installs. [OSHA NRTL program](https://www.osha.gov/nationally-recognized-testing-laboratory-program); [current NRTLs](https://www.osha.gov/nationally-recognized-testing-laboratory-program/current-list-of-nrtls). Field evaluation labels are site-/product-safety approvals, generally not centrally aggregated with robot-type/site/use fields. No deployment series. **GP-specific: no. Autonomy-aware: no.**
- **EU CE / Machinery Regulation:** conformity assessment and CE marking are manufacturer obligations; CE is not an EU installation/operation register. Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 Jan 2027, but it does not create a public robot-operating-hours database. [Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1230/oj). **No recurring deployment metric.**
- **China CR / compulsory certification:** no public national humanoid-specific CR register with operational status or work data located. Certification/safety registration is not evidence of actual workplace use. (Potential certification list is not equivalent to an adoption dataset.)

These records could corroborate market readiness, not measure deployment; a registry would need commissioning and periodic active-operation reporting.

## 7. Trade/customs data

No dedicated, stable international HS code for GP humanoids was located. Industrial robots often fall under HS **8479.50** (industrial robots, not elsewhere specified); other machines may appear in broad 8479.89 or other headings, while toys/demonstration models can be classified elsewhere. US CBP CROSS illustrates classification depends on product/function: [CROSS ruling H050116](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/H050116). Therefore customs counts of “humanoid” imports from broad codes are not reliable. A 2026 claim of Chinese humanoid exports under 84798970 (10,400 units/$717.7m H1) is **unverified classification/attribution**; do not use it as robot deployments without customs ruling and shipment-level validation. Customs captures cross-border goods, misses domestic China sales and says nothing about deployment, purpose, autonomy, or hours. **GP-specific: no; autonomy-aware: no.** Potential ancillary supply-chain signal only.

## 8. Telemetry and work-hours disclosures

- **Agility Digit:** claim of **65,000+ real-world operation hours** (cumulative) as of Sep 2026; source company announcement above. Best direct public number found. **Unverified independently;** ambiguous whether total across robots/customer sites, autonomous productive hours vs test/teleop, and exact end date. It is not an annual time series.
- **Figure:** BMW customer project is disclosed, but no public fleet-hour metric located. Any online numbers without company/customer primary-source trace remain **unverified**.
- **Dyna / other fleet-data vendors:** no recurring public disclosure of verified production fleet hours was found. “Dyna” identity/claim as given is **unverified**; do not quote fleet-hour numbers until tied to a primary source.
- **Other robot vendors:** Amazon has stated that it has deployed over one million robots in fulfillment operations, but this is predominantly fixed/warehouse-purpose-built equipment, not GP robots; not autonomy-adjusted work-hours. [Amazon/GeekWire coverage](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/amazon-robot-workforce-hits-1-million-heres-what-they-all-do/).

Telemetry has the right unit—hours, tasks, intervention—if an external auditor verifies logs. It lacks common definitions, coverage and a disclosed denominator. Recommended standard: quarterly active customer fleet, robot-days, productive autonomous hours, task completions/throughput, uptime, minutes of human assistance/remote control and safety incidents, separated by demo/pilot/paid production; report all robots and customer sites, not selected showcases.

## Recommendation for the Five Grand Endeavors KPI

**Do not publish a single shipment/adoption percentage as “work done.”** Until a survey or audited telemetry series exists, publish a clearly labeled **deployment evidence dashboard** (not a synthetic numeric KPI): (a) reported active productive robot-hours with verified scope; (b) active customer-operated GP units and sites, excluding pilots/research; (c) independently sourced, fulfilled tenders / shipments by end use; and (d) broad employer adoption survey percentages separately, with definitions and sample weights. State explicitly that no national work-time share is yet measurable.

Commission a compact annual Census/BTOS or DMP module with stable wording, sample weights and three-way stage: **demo / pilot / deployed production**. For deployed production, ask whether robot can execute a named physical task without continuous teleoperation; number of robots; operating hours in reference week; share of those hours under direct control, remote intervention and autonomous execution; tasks/output; industry/site; GP platform vs fixed-purpose equipment; and whether hours replace or assist human work. Publish questionnaire and cross-tabs; acquire secure microdata for research where necessary. Parallel protocol with Agility/UBTECH/Figure/customer filings and tenders can calibrate nonresponse and validate reports.

### Confidence and explicit gaps

High confidence in absence of a mature public autonomy-aware national series, and in Census/Eurostat/IFR limitations. Medium confidence in analyst shipment values and company claims (source definitions/independent audit vary). Low confidence in tender totals, customs classifications, vendor telemetry beyond explicitly quoted primary-source claims, and job-posting levels. No unsupported extrapolation from orders, shipments, revenue, AI adoption or announced pilots to work-hours is made here.
