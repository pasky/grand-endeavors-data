# Beyond check: “dark factories” — evidence review

**Cutoff:** 18 October 2026 (requested). “Dark factory” is a media/industry shorthand, not a consistently defined official statistic. It can refer to a particular process, line, shift, or site; it does not inherently mean no people are employed or physically present anywhere on site. Lighthouse certification is a smart-manufacturing recognition, not a lights-out certification. Evidence below distinguishes reported/claimed throughput from verified output and production capacity.

## Findings by claim

### Xiaomi Changping smartphone plant: 1 phone/second, no workers

**What is documented:** Beijing’s official science-and-technology portal, reporting Xiaomi founder Lei Jun’s 8 July 2024 announcement, describes the Changping “second phase” smartphone factory as opened, RMB 2.4bn investment, 81,000 m², rated annual capacity of 10 million flagship phones. It says “most” work would be done autonomously by smart robots, 24-hour continuous operation and an *average daily output* of 30,000 phones; it explicitly says all lines were planned to be in service by end-2024 and only then reach the 10m annual capacity. 30,000/day is 1.25m per 42-day month; spread over 365 days it is 10.95m/year, close to the headline annual capacity but not evidence of actual all-year output. Ten million/year corresponds to ~27,400/day or 0.87 phone/sec if 24/7. Thus “one phone per second” is an approximate capacity-derived slogan, not independently measured sustained throughput. The same official account reports the EV plant separately, avoiding a site conflation.

**People/scope:** “Most work” and round-the-clock running are documented, but no headcount, no claim of a fully empty site, and no statement that maintenance or technicians are absent. The numbers concern the factory, but its plant-wide unattended status is not established; claims “no workers” / “zero human presence” go beyond the source. The source documents industrial automation/robots, not humanoids or general-purpose mobile manipulators. No independent audit of actual output or human presence found. Source is governmental repost of the company-founder announcement, not independent verification.

Source: [Beijing International Science & Technology Innovation Center / Changping official account, 9 July 2024](https://www.ncsti.gov.cn/kjdt/scyq/wlkxc/wldt/202407/t20240709_170856.html).

### Xiaomi EV plant: “one car every 76 seconds”

The same official report describes six workshops (stamping, die-casting, body, paint, battery, assembly), >700 production robots, and “100% automation” for key processes, not the entire plant or every stage. It says a new SU7 could roll off every 76 seconds **when capacity is fully ramped**—a line-rate/capacity statement, not actual average output or a claim about unattended operation. Human staffing is not enumerated. No general-purpose robots are documented in this cited source. No independent verification of the 76-second rate found. Do not apply the phone-factory “dark” claim to the EV facility.

Source: [same official Beijing report](https://www.ncsti.gov.cn/kjdt/scyq/wlkxc/wldt/202407/t20240709_170856.html).

### SANY Beijing drilling-rig plant / “lighthouse”

This is unusually well documented and emphatically **not** a no-human plant. Xinhua reporting carried by People’s Daily in October 2021 describes a 4-hectare workshop with 12 automated lines and 150 robots; workers are trained as technicians. The plant’s headcount reportedly fell from 2,000 to one-fifth (~400), while annual equipment output grew from 1,000 to 3,000 units. This is a before/after site-level claim, not shift staffing or a measured unattended line-rate. The article reports an 85% rise in labor productivity and 77% shorter lead time; SANY’s own November 2021 account instead says productivity +185% and lead time −77% (30 to 7 days), illustrating metric/reporting discrepancies. The automations are conventional industrial robots, AGVs and machine vision, not general-purpose humanoids. The WEF lighthouse designation is independently confirmable as an award/selection; staffing/output claims derive from company executives quoted by Xinhua, not an audit. “Lighthouse” does not mean “lights out.”

Sources: [Xinhua via People’s Daily, 20 October 2021](https://en.people.cn/n3/2021/1020/c90000-9909459.html); [SANY, 15 November 2021](https://www.sanyglobal.com/press_releases/721/).

### SANY Changsha No. 18 / “entire factory”

SANY’s No. 18 plant is promoted as a lighthouse factory, but accessible company materials reviewed did not substantiate that the whole site runs without workers, give shift staffing or independently verified output. No evidence that general-purpose robots are deployed there. Do not infer lights-out status from the WEF label or promotional tour. **Status: lighthouse claim, not evidence of whole-plant unattended production.**

### Gree Gaolan air-conditioner plant: 12m/year, “world’s largest” lights-out

A Mobile World Live page carries what it labels a press release by China Unicom Guangdong/Gree. It calls the Gaolan factory the world’s largest 5.5G-native “lights-out” factory; says annual split-AC output is >12m and efficiency +86% versus a traditional factory; and says “a few employees” handle equipment maintenance, quality control and inspection. It also describes personnel in a control center monitoring data/alarms, 12 lines covered by cloud inspection, and workforce skills/HMI. These facts support highly automated operation with retained human monitoring and maintenance—not an empty plant or no workers. The 12m is annual output/capacity as asserted by the release, no actual period/output denominator is given; no independent audit. 5.5G/AI/industrial automation are not general-purpose robots. The page is explicitly a press release, and “world’s largest” is an unverified superlative.

Source: [Mobile World Live, 27 November 2024 (press release)](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/u-joy-cities-the-worlds-largest-5-5g-native-lights-out-factory/).

A separate 2025 People’s Daily article says a Gree smart factory makes an air conditioner every 15 seconds and describes >800 quality checks. The host URL returned 404 in this research, so I do **not** treat it as a cited/verified record and do not combine it with Gaolan’s 12m/year figure.

### CATL: lighthouse factories

CATL’s own account of its Sichuan/Yibin plant being selected as a WEF lighthouse explicitly describes pack lines as 80% automated and says an automated gluing line reduced manpower by 70%; production achieved 99.8% qualified rate. It highlights AI inspection and 40,000 sensors. This is process automation, not a claim of zero staff or whole-plant lights-out; no count of retained technicians/shifts or plant throughput is provided. No general-purpose robots documented. WEF recognition independently corroborates lighthouse status, not “dark factory” operation. CATL’s Liyang 2023 output/cost improvements (WEF case study snippet reports +320% output and −33% cost) likewise measure manufacturing improvement, not unattended operation; avoid treating percentage uplift as a plant capacity or zero-worker fact.

Sources: [CATL, 11 October 2022](https://www.catl.com/en/news/1030.html); [WEF Liyang case-study page](https://initiatives.weforum.org/global-lighthouse-network/case-study-details/catl---liyang/aJYTG00000001c14AA) (page accessible, but text extraction returned only cookie content; CATL’s 2023 release also reports the figures: [CATL, 14 December 2023](https://www.catl.com/en/news/6176.html)).

### FANUC Japan: 30-day lights-out / robots build robots

The strongest recurring account located is the Association for Manufacturing Technology (AMT), an industry association: FANUC has operated a “lights-out factory” since 2001; it makes about 50 robots per 24-hour shift and can run unsupervised for up to 30 days. This is a specific FANUC robot-manufacturing operation, not evidence that all FANUC sites or all production are lights-out. “Unsupervised for 30 days” describes a maximum unattended interval, not proof that no technicians or staff exist on site at other times; AMT gives no headcount or maintenance/shift description. FANUC industrial robots building robots are purpose-built industrial automation, not general-purpose robots. Independent corroboration is limited: AMT republishes the account without an independent site audit; an academic paper on FANUC’s 720-hour unattended continuous manufacturing system exists, but its abstract is the only accessible corroboration located. The 50/24h number is an approximate per-day production rate, not annual throughput or rated total capacity.

Sources: [AMT, 6 May 2021](https://www.amtonline.org/article/lights-out-manufacturing); [academic paper on FANUC’s 720-hour unattended system, 2008](https://www.emerald.com/ria/article/28/2/115/4945/Direct-input-and-output-system-another-secret).

### Philips Drachten, Netherlands: 128 robots / “nine humans”

The New York Times’ 2012 on-site report describes the Drachten shaver factory as having 128 robot arms, working three shifts a day, 365 days a year, and “several dozen workers per shift”—not nine people total or zero workers. It gives no actual throughput/capacity and does not claim a human-free shift or whole-site lights-out. The arms are dedicated assembly equipment, not general-purpose robots. This is independent reporting, but not an audit of unattended operation; the often viral “nine humans” figure is not supported by this cited article and appears to be a conflation in secondary summaries.

Source: [New York Times, 18 August 2012](https://www.nytimes.com/2012/08/19/business/new-wave-of-adept-robots-is-changing-global-industry.html).

### Foxconn/Kunshan: “replaced 60,000 workers with robots”

The 2016 viral claim has a narrower origin: South China Morning Post quoted a Kunshan official saying a factory reduced its *employee strength* from 110,000 to 50,000 “thanks to the introduction of robots.” It does not say that 60,000 robots replaced 60,000 people, that the plant became fully automated, or that all jobs were replaced directly by robots; it gives no production output, line/shift scope or remaining technician count. Foxconn reportedly said elsewhere that several factories had reduced labor through automation, but no verified “lights-out Foxconn plant” was identified. This is a workforce reduction claim, not lights-out evidence. No general-purpose robot claim. Independent verification of the precise causal attribution and figure was not found.

Sources: [SCMP, 21 May 2016](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/economy/article/1949918/rise-robots-60000-workers-culled-just-one-factory-chinas); [MarketWatch’s contemporaneous summary, 28 May 2016](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/foxconn-replaces-60000-humans-with-robots-in-china-2016-05-25). Treat as a reported official quote, not measured automation displacement.

### Haier, Midea, BYD, Huawei and Lenovo

- **Haier:** Searches surfaced general “smart factory” and lighthouse claims, but no strong source establishing an entire named Haier plant operates unattended, with a human staffing/shift account and output. Avoid “Haier dark factory” as a fact absent a named line/site and specific source.
- **Midea:** Digital industrial park sources document automated warehouses, logistics corridors and smart production. A WEF/lighthouse label or “robotic production” is not evidence of lights-out. No reliable whole-site lights-out, staffing or throughput claim substantiated in sources reviewed. Dedicated automation; no general-purpose robots established.
- **BYD:** Viral “dark factory”/automation narratives lacked primary evidence for a named plant’s unattended scope, shift staffing or actual capacity. Foxconn’s 60,000 figure is not BYD evidence. No verified general-purpose-robot role found.
- **Huawei:** No primary or reputable source located for the circulated categorical claim of a Huawei fully dark factory. Smart manufacturing/5G deployments are not sufficient.
- **Lenovo:** WEF’s Hefei case study identifies a Global Lighthouse site and more than 30 advanced automation/analytics use cases, but does not designate it lights-out or supply whole-plant unattended staffing. “World’s largest single PC factory” is a size description, not a darkness claim. No general-purpose robots documented. WEF recognition is independent for lighthouse status only.

Sources: [WEF Lenovo Hefei case-study page](https://initiatives.weforum.org/global-lighthouse-network/case-study-details/lenovo---hefei/aJY68000000Kz4VGAS) (page opened; text extraction was cookie-limited); [Midea digital park company source](https://mbt.midea.com/global/news/--midea-with-advanced-technology-embracing-a-digital-intelligenc).

## Chinese government statistics and terminology

There is **no official national count of “黑灯工厂” / “无人工厂”** in the government sources reviewed, and no evidence that “dark factory” is a defined MIIT statistical category. The state program is **智能工厂梯度培育** (“smart factory gradient cultivation”), with four levels—基础级 (basic), 先进级 (advanced), 卓越级 (excellent), 领航级 (pioneer)—not a count of worker-free plants. The six-department 2025 notice requires a site to progress through tiers; advanced and above concern digitalization/network collaboration/intelligence, while “lighthouse/dark” is not the qualifying standard. Basic/advanced are locally evaluated; excellent/pioneer are nationally organized. Therefore the counts in tier lists cannot be quoted as “dark factories.”

MIIT’s September 2025 notice publishes a **proposed** 2025 excellent-level list for public comment; it is not a dark-factory count. A 2025 official notice reports 15 first “领航级” cultivation sites (a program cohort), not 15 dark factories. The 2025 guidance caps recommendations by province according to prior-year awards, further indicating selection cohorts rather than an exhaustive national census. Lower-tier tallies cited by secondary industry content (e.g., “35,000 basic / 7,000 advanced / 500 excellent”) should not be repeated without a dated primary MIIT dataset and its counting method.

Sources: [Six-department 2025 smart factory policy notice](https://gxt.fujian.gov.cn/jdhy/zxzcfg/gjzcfg/202507/t20250703_6961389.htm); [MIIT 2025 excellent-level proposed list notice, 5 September 2025](https://www.ncsti.gov.cn/kjdt/tzgg/202509/t20250905_218245.html); [2025 pioneer cohort notice/report, 1 December 2025](https://gxt.fujian.gov.cn/zwgk/xw/hydt/xydt/202512/t20251201_7040916.htm).

## Editorial rules for the “Beyond” section

1. Use “lights-out” only for a stated autonomous interval/process; identify exact plant/line/shift and source attribution. Distinguish it from whole-site emptiness.
2. Preserve qualifiers: “most work,” “key processes,” “at full ramp,” “can run unsupervised up to,” and “a few maintenance staff” are not equivalent to “no workers.”
3. Put rated annual capacity, claimed daily output, actual measured output, and line cycle time in separate fields. Annualize only with explicit operating-day assumptions; a theoretical conversion is not production data.
4. Lighthouse status, “smart factory,” 5G, robots, and highly automated lines do not prove lights-out operation. Ordinary fixed industrial arms/AGVs are not general-purpose robots.
5. Count no official “dark factories” unless an authoritative source defines the category and census method. MIIT smart-factory program figures are a different denominator.
